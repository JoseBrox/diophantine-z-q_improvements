## Finite exceptions without another witness coordinate

The [power-shift theorem](../papers/number_fields/power_shifts_and_finite_exceptions_20261117_v69.tex) uses distinct completely split primes and a Chinese remainder construction. For any finite subset `T` of a number field and any fixed exponent `m ≥ 2`, one can choose an integer shift `B`, in any prescribed integer progression, such that no element of `T` lies in `K^m+B`.

Suppose a polynomial of degree at most `D` in `N` total coordinates defines a subset of a degree-`d` number field that contains every sufficiently large positive rational integer and has only finitely many nonintegral elements. Substitution of `X²+B` gives a set containing every rational integer and contained in the ring of integers, in the same `N` coordinates. An integral basis, elimination of its last coefficient and a degree-`d` field norm then define the ring of integers by a single equation in **`dN` total coordinates**, of degree at most **`2dD`**. This count includes every witness and the input.

With finite error relative to the rational integers themselves, a suitable power shift defines the rational integers in the original `N` coordinates. The exponent condition `gcd(m,d)=1` together with rationality of the mth roots of unity in the field suffices. A square works exactly when the field has no quadratic subfield, including every odd-degree field; its degree bound is `2D`, also in the eventual-positive version. For odd powers the latter version uses a two-sign product with one shared witness tuple.

The [exact checks](../computation/number_fields/power_shifts_v69) include reproducible split-prime avoidance examples for exponents two, three and five. The [independent proof review](audits/sun_v2_20261117_v69/independent_power_shift_review.md) checks the general argument and degree/count bounds. The theorem does not construct a detector over all number fields, and the sufficient shift can be ineffective when the finite exceptional set is not known effectively.

## Arithmetic restrictions on the actual covers

The [actual-trace theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/curves_and_abelian_families_20261117_v69.tex) concerns the existing guarded actual-trace loci at a fixed rational input. All three point roles in the derivative source, and all nine in the specified general source, may vary independently.

- Every fixed algebraic curve in either locus has only finitely many rational points. No geometrically integral curve over the rationals whose normalization has genus zero or one maps nonconstantly into it.
- With the point tuple fixed, the derivative cover has at least eighteen simple branch contacts and genus at least eight. The first general cover has ten simple branch contacts and genus at least four.
- Every abelian family in the general source has constant common slope. The derivative statement also fixes the root point.
- The derivative fixed-point fiber has two rational points at infinity and is everywhere locally soluble. The infinity points and the eighteen branch contacts are outside the guarded affine source.

The proof combines the rational simplicity of the Weil-restriction surface, divisorial parity, an unramified double cover of an abelian variety, and Faltings's theorem. Trace cancellation cannot defeat the divisorial argument. Zero comparison tests and the stated finite menu of small polynomial multipliers are included. The [independent mathematical review](../negative_results/computation/actual_trace_curves_v69/independent_review.md) and [exact algebra checks](../negative_results/computation/actual_trace_curves_v69) have separate scopes.

These statements do not exclude individual rational witnesses or prove failure of either realization criterion. The arithmetic problem is still to find guarded rational points at the actual trace for every sufficiently large positive input, simultaneously in the two-detector case. The targets **21 and 9 remain unproved**.

## The attempted 194-coordinate chart

The [search ledger](../computation/quartic/search194_v69) records a proposed storage count `195−42+36+4+1=194`, with three six-field point charts, two reciprocal-square helpers and one additional monomial unit. The original five units were fixed. All 32 additional units quadratically definable from them, the stated factor assignments and three helper-role models were included; their integer scaling weights were unbounded. All three models returned `unsat`.

A separate standard-library checker finds the incompatibility already in two factors. For each extra unit, none of the four required exponent differences occurs in `(P+P)−(P+P)`. It checks all 128 cases without an SMT solver. This also explains why further permutations within those unit coordinates cannot resolve the obstruction.

No smaller polynomial follows. The result does not cover different original unit coordinates, nonmonomial helpers, arbitrary equation bases or other quartic constructions. Changing those coordinates is a concrete next algebraic target.
