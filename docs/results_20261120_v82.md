### Partial trace and a smaller point chart

In the retained field $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, write $f=1/x(H)$ and use the positive scalar $R$. The new equations are

$$
J=\frac{Z^2}{R^5}+\frac{f^2}{R^4},\qquad
\tau\operatorname{Tr}(Z^2/R^5)=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(J),\qquad
b_s=R(R+9792)\tau^2.
$$

At a consumed odd scalar valuation $\mu=v_p(R)>0$, the native pole implies, in one common place above $p$,

$$
v(Z)>\frac94\mu,\qquad v(f)>\frac74\mu.
$$

Positivity excludes both $Z=0$ and a zero partial trace. With $\widehat J=R^4J$, $L=\widehat J-f^2$, and $\widehat\tau=R^4\tau$, the scalar equations become the quadrics

$$
RL=Z^2,\qquad \widehat\tau\operatorname{Tr}(L)=N(\widehat J).
$$

Scaling the scalar point chart by $Z$ gives the linear identity $M=-2S-W$. Both rational components of $M$ can therefore be deleted. The inverse point map is proved on every rational solution; the primary denominator is forced nonzero, whereas the alternate zero-ordinate branch remains allowed.

In the general family, the aggregate uses $R x(Q_2)$ instead of $x(Q_2)$. The retained coordinate $P^*$ already equals this product, so no reciprocal of $R$ is needed. Four actual input rows force

$$
\rho=r(X)>0,\qquad R(1+149^2\rho)=149C^2\rho.
$$

This establishes $R>0$ before using any Laurent inverse and excludes an extra component at $R=0$. The fixed member retains its unweighted aggregate and one inverse coordinate. The net changes are consequently $165\to163$ and $180\to179$.

**The shared $R^2$ coordinate remains.** The native relation $\widehat\tau\sigma=R^4$ is represented by $\widehat\tau\sigma=R_2^2$, $R_2=R^2$. This construction does not claim the requested deletion of that register.

### Comparison precision and soundness

The scaled comparison is

$$
E_2^*=A\overline{(V+M)}+2\overline{T_H}\,\overline Z
      =-\overline{Zt_2}\,C_2.
$$

Its fourth-power term receives exponent 11; the companion term retains exponent 5. The scalar label $a$ compensates for the potentially nonintegral multiplier. The proof establishes

$$
v_p\bigl(a(A-a)\bigr)\ge3,\qquad M_j^3\mid a\Delta,
$$

before transferring integrality to the other comparisons. Since $|a\Delta|\le14B_0^5$, the retained radical-height argument gives exponent $35/18<2$.

For 163 variables, the [positive weighted trace theorem](../papers/trace_heights/weighted_trace_20261114_v27.tex), applied to fixed weights $(1,r,1,1)$, gives

$$
B_0^2\le A\bigl(h(R)+h(T)\bigr)+J.
$$

The constants are independent of the fixed family parameter $N$. The underlying published result is [von Buhren, Theorem 1.1, p. 308](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2017__29_1_307_0.pdf); the repository proof checks its projective embedding and exceptional-translate hypotheses. Its additive constant is ineffective.

Set $L_N=\lfloor9(2N-1)/4\rfloor+1$. The scalar forces $B_0\ge|a|\ge149^{L_N}$ and $h(\beta)\le K_0 149^{-L_N}|a|$. Together with

$$
h(R)\le4h(\beta)+(2N+1)\log149+\log1801500,
$$

this makes the extra scalar-height term uniformly $o(B_0^2)$. Fix the height constants first, absorb that term, use $35/18<2$ to bound $B_0$ independently of $N$, and only then enlarge $N$. The input argument and Lucas separation finish the proof. No effective threshold is asserted.

## A new elliptic divisibility detector

The [complete proof](../papers/elliptic_divisibility/prepared_eds_divisibility_20261120_v82.tex) and [exact checks](../computation/variable_reductions_v82/eds/README.md) use

$$
E:y^2=x^3-2x,\qquad
b(H,U)=\frac{y(H)+x(U)^2(1+x(U)^2)}{(1+x(U)^2)^2}.
$$

For $U=[m]H$, negative valuations of this expression are even outside a fixed finite set. At a primitive denominator prime where $H$ has odd formal depth and $U$ is nonformal, its valuation is negative and odd. Ordinary rational abc supplies an eventual primitive exponent-one divisor; the manuscript also gives a direct proof with positive mass margin $77/303>1/5$, following the denominator argument in [Cornelissen–Zahidi, Proposition 3.10(iii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412473).

For every nonzero integral quotient, an ordered real and finite-place choice of $H$ prepares $d=(112/841)b$ for the published intrinsic pole theorem. Two one-coordinate point charts, two pole parameters, and a rank-eight finite-algebra encoder give **five coordinates for this prepared divisibility subrelation**. The [LMFDB record 256.b1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/256/b/1) supplies the recorded generator assertion; exact PARI checks independently recover rank and torsion.

This is not a five-variable definition of the integers. It need not accept every preassigned divisible pair, and it does not identify an external input $X$ with $j/h$. That identification is the remaining constructive task.

## A sharper ten-coordinate criterion and a global obstruction

The [genus-one manuscript](../papers/pole_parameters/genus_one_sound_criterion_20261120_v82.tex) considers the actual source functions

$$
d=\frac{7u}{(1+u)^2},\qquad H=V^2+d,
\qquad Y^2=d(s^4-H^2).
$$

At a positive odd contact $\mu=v_p(u)$, this equation forces $2v_p(V)<\mu$, exactly the scalar inequality needed by the existing soundness proof. A non-torsion rational point at an actual prepared detector tuple for each integer would replace two free pole parameters by one and give ten total coordinates. **That actual-input rational-point assertion remains unproved.**

A sharper sufficient target is now proved: for one fixed admissible source constant, it is enough to realize these actual fibers at all but finitely many inputs $cm^2+b$, with fixed positive squarefree $c$ and $b\in\mathbb Z$. Substitute $T=cX^2+b$ in the sound ten-coordinate polynomial. If $X=a/q$ is reduced and $T$ is integral, then $q^2\mid c$, so $q=1$. Finitely many missing integer inputs are restored by multiplying their linear factors. This preserves the ten-coordinate count. No such progression is yet known to have the required actual rational witnesses.

Every prepared scalar fiber is nevertheless locally soluble at every place. The proof covers negative and positive valuations, the prime two, and the real place. This stronger local conclusion is still insufficient for global realization.

An exact counterexample uses

$$
u=\frac{6137}{256},\quad
d=\frac{10997504}{40870449},\quad
V=\frac{2698}{2131},\quad
e=\frac{4d}{V^2}=\frac{30464}{45369}.
$$

These values have all stated real, denominator, and two-adic preparation properties, with nonsquare $u$. Scaling identifies the associated elliptic curve with $E_{6305215}$. Certified algebraic descent gives rank exactly one. Its full rational Kummer image forces equal valuation parity at 17 and 10597 for every nonzero shifted abscissa $x,x-N,x+N$. The required square class 119 has unequal parity. The obstruction survives every constant multiplier supported on $\{2,3,5,7\}$ and every power of $N$.

The [read-only checker](../computation/variable_reductions_v82/abc/check_genus_one.py) reconstructs the three curve maps, both isogenous algebraic-rank bounds, all eight Kummer classes, and the exact prepared tuple. It uses PARI's algebraic bounds, not an analytic-rank or BSD inference. The second descent uses the same PARI implementation and is not presented as an independent implementation. The counterexample is **not asserted to occur at the actual contact detector or actual trace**.

The [separate independent review](../computation/variable_reductions_v82/abc/independent_review/proof_review.md) reconstructs the Kummer image using actual group additions and checks the entire 64-class target family. It also verifies the quadratic-progression corollary. The [common-level scope note](../computation/variable_reductions_v82/abc/common_level_scope.md) proves that one rank-one congruent-number curve of level $n\equiv5\pmod8$ cannot provide two distinct positive nonsquare abscissa classes both prepared in one fixed 2-adic class $1$ or $7$. This excludes that naive way of sharing the general construction’s two pole levels; sharing a different parameter remains open.
