# Bounded-variable undecidability: v89

## Proved unrestricted-degree transfer

The [complete TeX proof](../undecidability/papers/transfer_general_degree.tex)
and [PDF](../undecidability/papers/transfer_general_degree.pdf) prove that a
polynomial definition of the integers in `m` total rational coordinates gives
undecidability of rational solvability in **`9m+2` unknowns**.

| Source | Source coordinates, including input | Rational unknowns in the decision problem |
|:--|--:|--:|
| General few-variable family | 25 | **227** |
| Fully specified general member | 27 | **245** |
| Ordinary rational abc family | 11 | **101** |

The first two need no additional abc hypothesis. Every application retains
the source's explicit arithmetic premises. The general and abc defining
parameters are ineffective; a fixed finite defining coefficient list suffices
for the existence form of undecidability, but an implemented reduction must
use a certified defining member. Small stored fixtures are not such members.

Sun's Theorem 1.1(i) represents any recursively enumerable set using eight
arbitrary integer unknowns and one nonnegative integer unknown. The identity

```math
L(s,t,u)=s^2+t^2+u^2+u
```

represents every nonnegative integer with integral witnesses and is at least
`-1/4` over the rationals. Its integer values are therefore nonnegative.
Substituting `L` directly into the universal polynomial and into the ninth
integer predicate leaves `8+3+9(m-1)=9m+2` rational unknowns. Summing the ten
squared equations proves both directions without new arithmetic hypotheses.

The general mixed-domain formula is `(r+s)m+2s` for `r` unrestricted integer
unknowns and `s` nonnegative integer unknowns. The instance parameter is part
of the coefficients, not another existential unknown.

## Explicit degree and its provenance

Using Bayer--David--Haßler--Matiyasevich--Schleicher, version 3 of
22 October 2026, and Jones's proved universal pair `(58,4)`, all three routes
have degree at most

```text
3362086470453239832602365249023837055668275467414816896671079680
```

This is `2*eta(58,4)`, and is larger than four times each integer predicate's
degree. The authors explicitly distinguish this from the better degree using
`(32,12)`, for which Jones supplied no proof. We preserve that qualification.
The theorem's variable count only needs Sun's published mixed-domain result.
We do not claim to have rerun the external Isabelle development.

## Further proved limitations

The manuscript proves that fewer than `r` homogeneous integer-valued tests
cannot characterize integrality of arbitrary rational `r`-tuples: for
infinitely many primes there are nonintegral tuples with all test values
integral. This uses a common projective algebraic zero, primes admitting a
local embedding, and exact congruence approximation. It does not exclude
systems, inhomogeneous tests, or jointly constructed arithmetic witnesses.

An explicit rational counterexample to a relation-combining divisibility
claim shows why simply omitting the ninth integer certificate is unsound.
It is a counterexample to that proposed general lemma, not to the full
universal polynomial's arithmetic construction.

## Quartic source audit remains separate

The earlier **2,539 / 2,779 / 934** candidates were first committed as a
[dated checkpoint](../undecidability/checkpoints/baseline_20261123.md).
The independent reconstruction checks the algebraic 62-variable/50-row to
57-variable/45-row transformation of the printed Mishchenko--Treier system.
However, its final Pell equation has a missing-square discrepancy relative
to the classical construction. Source universality is not established by
the finite algebraic check. Those counts therefore remain under audit.

The unrestricted-degree result does not prove a 263-variable quartic.
Direct quadratic compilation and alternative primary-source systems continue
as a separate research task.
