## Effective abscissa-sum heights over every number field

The [height theorem](../papers/effective_sums/elliptic_sum_heights_over_number_fields_20261117_v63.tex) applies to every elliptic curve over a number field K and points Q_i=[n_i]P+T_i in one cyclic group modulo K-rational torsion. For finite Q_i, M=max(1,|n_i|), and integral c, it gives

```math
h\left(\sum_i x(Q_i)+c\right)
\ge\max_i h(x(Q_i))-O_{E,K,P,r}(\log(M+2)),
```

with an effective constant. Negative and zero indices, repetitions and all rational torsion translates are included. The finite-place loss is explicit. Its ramification term is at most log|disc(K)|/[K:Q]. The infinite-place estimate uses David–Hirata-Kohno's published elliptic-logarithm theorem, with its linear dependence on coefficient height.

The theorem gives effective finiteness of S-integral abscissa sums in the fixed cyclic group. Zywina's rank-one existence theorem, cited in its preprint form, supplies curves over every number field for which the estimate controls the whole finite K-point domain. The estimate concerns algebraic sums; it does not identify them with field traces.

The [finite-exception criterion](../papers/number_fields/finite_exceptions_and_integrality_20261117_v63.tex) isolates a weaker sufficient detector target. A Diophantine set containing all but finitely many rational integers and having bounded-height nonintegral elements suffices to define O_K. Finite correction gives a set between Z and O_K, and an integral basis gives the whole ring. The paper supplies exact coordinate and degree bounds for these operations. It does not construct the required detector or effectively identify its finite exceptional set.

## Restrictions on two proposed routes

The [contact-coupling obstruction](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/six_contact_mobius_obstruction_20261117_v63.tex) covers every fixed integer exponent at least four and every fixed finite menu of such exponents. All twelve rational Möbius symmetries are accounted for. Ten branches fail a uniform square-gap test, one violates a guard, and the last has inert-prime obstructions on a common progression of coefficient shifts. This rules out eventual prescribed-index completeness for that displayed six-contact architecture. It does not exclude the derivative construction, arbitrary nonunit rescalings, or every nine-variable construction.

The [rank-stable reduction obstruction](../negative_results/papers/number_fields/rank_stable_adjacent_contacts_20261117_v63.tex) shows why a rank-one curve defined over K and remaining rank one over L cannot directly replace the original curve in the adjacent-index conjugate detector. Once [t]P descends to K, contacts at h and h-c above the same base prime force the reduction of [ct]P to vanish. For a fixed nonzero c this occurs at only finitely many primes. Torsion, ramification and residue characteristics dividing t are included.

Neither obstruction gives a counterexample to the assertion that every number-field ring of integers is Diophantine in its fraction field. The outstanding constructive requirements are local integrality extraction, integer completeness and simultaneous rational witnesses over the chosen field.
