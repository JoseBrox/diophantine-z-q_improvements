```math

Q_B=\sum_{i=1}^{66}q_i^2,
```

where every $q_i$ is a primitive integral quadric. For each fixed odd
$B\ge81$, the polynomial has degree exactly four, integer content one,
1,695 nonzero coordinate monomials, and exact coefficient height

```math
H(Q_B)=\max\{30568,B^2+1\}.
```

simultaneous-prime, all-point trace-height and simultaneous-twist premises,
every sufficiently large fixed odd $B$ gives rational input projection

$T=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}x(U-V)$.
The height scale is $B+X^8+T^2$. At every consumed prime the
complete detector gives the integral-index residual $X(m-X)$;

## A complete quadratic graph for reference squares

The [inverse reference theorem](../papers/elliptic_charts/inverse_reference_squares_20261116_v48.tex)
works over any characteristic-zero field. Write
$\delta=a-c^2$, $D=a\delta$ and
$\Delta=(a+\delta)^2-4ac^2$, and assume $ac\delta\Delta\ne0$.
Use coordinates $u,w,h,A,B$ and the affine expression
$v=a(u+w)+2c+\delta h$. The five quadrics are

```math
\begin{aligned}
auw&=1,\\
\delta h^2+ah(u+w)+2ch&=1,\\
aA(v+\delta w)+2chu+h-u&=0,\\
aB(v+\delta u)+2chw+h-w&=0,\\
a^2\!\left[\delta^2AB-(1+4cw)A-(1+4cu)B\right]
       +1-2(2a+c^2)h^2&=0.
\end{aligned}
```

They give exactly the graph $A=u^2h^2$, $B=w^2h^2$ over the
complete three-coordinate reference chart. The first two equations
already make $u,w,h$ units. The two linear graph coefficients cannot
vanish together; when one vanishes, the final equation recovers the
corresponding square with a nonzero coefficient. Thus the graph includes
every exceptional fiber without an additional guard.

Both reference ratios are affine:

```math
S=aw+c+\delta h,\qquad T_H=-au-c-\delta h.
```

The graph consequently connects to every detector and ratio row in the
complete quartic. Its two square coordinates use the coordinate budget
saved by the smaller reference chart. This connection gives the
coefficient improvement above, while leaving the complete count at 74.
The [exact package](../computation/elliptic_charts/inverse_reference_squares_v48)
contains Laurent normal-form identities and an independent exhaustive
finite-field check of 10,840 base points, including both exceptional
graph types. The all-field statement is proved in the manuscript.

## Bounded detector-dependent complement levels

The [native norm-level theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/bounded_norm_levels_20261116_v48.tex)
applies to every general and conditional native detector, written

```math
W=\prod_k(1+w h_k^2),\qquad
N=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(W),\qquad
V=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(W^{-1}),\qquad
b=4w/V^2.
```

Outside the original exceptional prime set, an odd valuation of $w$
forces $v_p(N)\le0$. If this norm valuation is zero, then
$N\equiv1\pmod p$. Hence

```math
m=\frac{N}{1+N},\qquad a=17m^2
```

preserves the negative-odd-valuation implication required by the pole
detector. The theorem also allows finitely many fixed choices
$m=MN/(c+N)$, where $M$ is a positive integer, $c\ge0$ is
integral outside the exceptional set, and $1+c$ is a unit there.

At every character of a prepared actual point algebra, both real
values of $W$ are at least one. Thus $c+N$ and $m$ are units
of the entire point algebra. The complement is also a unit: its
vanishing would make $17/w$ a square in $K$, contrary to its odd
valuation at 17. These functions and guards require no additional
unrestricted coordinate in the finite-algebra encoding.

Consequently the **21/9 implication** remains valid if the two actual
complements can be realized in a fixed finite set of positive square
classes supported on the original exceptional primes. The theorem
does not assert this realization.

Writing the actual detector as $W=W_0+\rho W_1$, the exact required
equations are

```math
\left(\frac{2M_iW_{i,0}}{c_i+N_i}\right)^2-d_i y_i^2
       =\frac{4w_i}{17},\qquad i=0,1.
```

The first coordinates are prescribed by the two coupled native detector
tuples. For the canonical choice $M_i=c_i=1$, the additional identity

```math
u_i=\frac{2W_{i,0}}{1+N_i},\quad
v_i=\frac{2W_{i,1}}{1+N_i},\quad
z_i=\frac{1-N_i}{1+N_i},\qquad
u_i^2-2v_i^2+z_i^2=1
```

gives $0<u_i\le1$ and $17/4\le a_i<17$ on prepared real
characters. The ordered real preparation also permits $b_i<a_i$.
Bare norm-conic solubility, this quadric identity and finite local
filters do not supply the simultaneous rational points with the
prescribed coordinates. That is the remaining arithmetic target.

## Separate obstructions

The [negative-results collection](../negative_results/README.md) includes
two precise restrictions relevant to these targets.

- The [automatic-level theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/automatic_level_finiteness_20261116_v48.tex)
  proves that $a=w\operatorname{Norm}(W)$ cannot supply uniform
  conditional completeness. Every guarded auxiliary tuple has a negative
  level valuation at a positive odd contact. Admissibility forces the
  argument onto finitely many genus-two twists; Faltings' theorem then
  gives finitely many possible arguments. Fixed finite rational
  rescalings do not repair this construction. The bounded levels above
  are outside this obstruction's scope.
- The [tangent-completion theorem](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/tangent_completion_20261116_v48.tex)
  gives exact false-height families and computes the entire quadratic
  identity space for the specified compressed pairing. All quadrics in
  its twenty rational components, including conjugate products, leave
  the false family. Adjoining the physical trace alone still leaves it
  with an arbitrary rational trace. Different embeddings, additional
  helpers and separately justified restricted witness families remain
  possible. This is not a global variable-count lower bound.
