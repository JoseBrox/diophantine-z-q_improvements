## Rank-one curves over general number fields

The [Pasten–Vidaux audit and amplified descent](../papers/number_fields/amplified_rationality_descent_20261119_v81.tex) provide a new conditional rational-subfield definition under abc over K and positive elliptic rank stable from Q to K. It handles all rational targets, both signs of odd valuation, and a fixed six-point divisor of a cubic rational map. This architecture uses the global conjugate difference t−σ(t), so it avoids the adjacent-contact obstruction below. It does not distinguish integers among rational inputs. Zywina supplies the needed stable curve for quadratic extensions; no such blanket conclusion is claimed for every higher-degree field.

The [effective sum-height theorem](../papers/effective_sums/elliptic_sum_heights_over_number_fields_20261117_v63.tex) now works for every elliptic curve over every number field. Together with Zywina's rank-one existence theorem it supplies whole-domain height control on suitable curves, without retaining the original complex-multiplication model. The remaining tasks are to construct the required local integrality detector over the chosen field, prove simultaneous rational realization on that field, and encode the complete witness set. The height theorem does not identify field traces with algebraic sums and does not establish a Diophantine definition of the ring of integers.

The [rank-stable contact obstruction](../negative_results/papers/number_fields/rank_stable_adjacent_contacts_20261117_v63.tex) excludes a direct use of a curve defined over the base field whose positive rank remains stable in the detector extension. Its adjacent contacts can occur at only finitely many primes. A new local architecture, or a curve defined over the extension without the stated descent property, is required.

The [power-shift criterion](../papers/number_fields/power_shifts_and_finite_exceptions_20261117_v69.tex) needs a detector containing every sufficiently large positive rational integer and having bounded-height nonintegral inputs. A fixed square shift removes the finite errors without another witness coordinate. An integral basis and a field norm then give the whole ring in `dN` total coordinates and degree at most `2dD`, for field degree `d` and an original polynomial of degree `D` in `N` coordinates. The shift can satisfy prescribed congruences even over complex fields. Constructing such a detector remains the arithmetic task.

The same height theorem also controls reciprocal abscissas on $E:y^2=x^3-Dx$, $D\ne0$. If $Q_i=[n_i]P+T_i$ are finite points in one cyclic group modulo torsion with $x(Q_i)\ne0$, and $M=\max(1,|n_i|)$, then effectively

$$
h\!\left(\sum_i\frac1{x(Q_i)}\right)
\ge \max_i h(x(Q_i))-C\log(M+2)-C'.
$$

Indeed, $T_0=(0,0)$ is rational two-torsion and $x(Q+T_0)=-D/x(Q)$. The translated points remain in the same cyclic group modulo torsion and are finite under the stated nonzero-abscissa condition. Applying the sum-height theorem to them, then using $h(1/x)=h(x)$ and $|h(Dx)-h(x)|\le h(D)$ twice, gives the inequality. The constants depend only on the fixed curve, field, generator and number of summands. This is a direct corollary useful for reciprocal charts; it neither controls field-trace cancellation nor supplies detector completeness.

## Infinitely many rationally soluble levels

For each prepared input $d$, the [varying-level theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/varying_level_pole_fibers_20261117_v61.tex) studies

```math
k(r)=(1-d)\frac{d+r^2}{d-r^2},\qquad
y^2=d\bigl(k(r)^2-s^4\bigr).
```

Every rational $r$ gives an everywhere locally soluble fiber. Under the retained simultaneous-twist theorem, infinitely many rational levels $0<r<\sqrt d$ have infinitely many rational points. The proof selects two fixed elliptic curves and varies their points independently; it identifies the required abscissa square class, rather than inferring it from positive rank. The combined witness set is Zariski dense and permits all-character guard avoidance, including characters at which $d=1$.

The cleared radicand has degree nine, twelve monomials and coefficient height four. For the nonsquare-complement input $d=112/225$, an exact example is

```math
r=\frac{44}{69},\quad k=\frac{3364}{675},\quad
s=\frac{29}{15},\quad y=\frac{23548}{10125}.
```

The theorem selects good levels; it does not realize a level prescribed by an existing detector coordinate. Both $r$ and $s$ remain free, and it does not synchronize two actual detector inputs. The construction counts are unchanged.

### Integral labels from one finite Pell choice

The [integral Lucas-label theorem](../papers/integer_labels/pell_labels_20261115_v40.tex) supplies a different arithmetic compression. Let $a,n$ be integers with

$$
|a|\ge3,\qquad c=|a-2|\ge3,\qquad n\ne0,\qquad t=(a-2)n+1.
$$

If a rational $w$ satisfies

$$
t^2-atw+w^2=1,
$$

then monicity forces $w$ to be an integer. With $U_0(A)=0$, $U_1(A)=1$ and $U_{j+1}(A)=AU_j(A)-U_{j-1}(A)$, the sharp lower bound is

$$
|t|\ge U_{c-1}(|a|)\ge(|a|-1)^{c-2}.
$$

Thus a third separately integral label can be replaced by a finite quadratic choice when the first two labels are already exact integers. The equivalent square condition is

$$
z^2=(a^2-4)\bigl((a-2)n+1\bigr)^2+4,
$$

of total degree six and coefficient height sixteen. The same paper gives sharp witnesses and arbitrarily large solutions preserving prescribed finite congruence data of a given witness.

The present source carries labels through local approximations by elliptic formal-parameter ratios. Those rational ratios are not known to be the exact integral labels, so monicity at the ratios does not prove the needed integrality. This distinction has a concrete counterexample: $a=5,n=1$ gives discriminant $340$, which is not a rational square, but for every $r\ge1$ there are rational norm-one solutions with $0<n_r<1$ and $n_r\equiv1\pmod{19^r}$. The separate [rational Pell approximation obstruction](../negative_results/papers/integer_labels/rational_pell_approximation_obstruction_20261116_v42.tex) proves this by an explicit rational parametrization. A useful application must therefore recover an exact integer carrier, or prove a new global implication from the complete source equations. Arbitrarily precise local approximation alone does not close the gap.

### Dependencies among point roles

A replacement for the nine points need not realize every independent tuple of labels. That freedom is where a genuine reduction can occur. It must nevertheless give witnesses for every integer input and prove soundness on every rational solution of the new source.

Two established obstructions delimit direct substitutions:

- The [dimension and label-graph theorems](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/structural_constraints_20261109_v13.tex) show that a finite algebraic presentation recovering arbitrary tuples of nine points needs at least eighteen rational parameters. The integer-index graph itself is Zariski dense in the product of the additive group and the relevant abelian variety.
- The [index-product theorem](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/index_products_and_finite_correspondences_20261110_v21.tex) shows that a fixed correspondence with finite geometric fibers cannot recover $[nh]P$ from $[n]P,[h]P$ uniformly for infinitely many $n$ and infinitely many permitted $h$ for each $n$. Restricting each index to congruence classes does not remove this obstruction.

The separate [affine scalar-coupling obstruction](../negative_results/papers/integer_labels/affine_scalar_coupling_obstruction_20261116_v50.tex) excludes every fixed finite set of identifications $S=[m]A+[k]P$ for the original general scalar source: scalar odd-contact divisibility contradicts those identities on an infinite integer progression. It does not cover a redesigned scalar or couplings involving other root roles.

These statements concern generic recovery and the specified finite correspondences. They do not exclude a smaller arithmetic source whose witnesses satisfy additional dependencies, or one using a different integer-growth relation.

Every coordinate is included in the complete count; nonzero input excludes zero contact weight, and zero input has a separate literal witness. The physical pole input is still $b=w(\operatorname{Norm}(W)/\operatorname{Tr}(W))^2$ and uses the retained simultaneous-twist premise.

The next concrete quartic target is to reduce the **two field product helpers and the rational half-trace output** while keeping both comparison errors affine and the original difference trace available with its controlled input scaling. Any replacement must preserve the unit proof at nonzero input and admit a complete zero-input witness. The [tangent-completion theorem](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/tangent_completion_20261116_v48.tex) excludes repairs by quadrics in its specified old compressed embedding, including after adjoining the physical trace. It does not exclude the variable-difference embedding used here or a further change of coordinates. A proposed saving must count every newly introduced product, square and inverse.

The general quartic needs deeper comparison precision than this ordinary-abc detector. The 194-coordinate construction supplies that precision through polynomial cross products and a homogeneous Lucas identity, together with a separate synchronized-depth completeness proof.

### Further elimination from the 194- and 229-variable systems

The [current general quartic](../papers/quartic/quartic194_20261117_v73.tex) has 194 coordinates, eight contact units and 168 quadratic rows. Its exact physical source and unit transformation provide the starting point for further deletion of implicit ratios or changes to the scalar chart. A favorable count for an isolated block is insufficient; all consumers and every unit boundary must remain part of the count and proof.

The [229-variable explicit construction](../papers/quartic/quartic229_compact_20261117_v68.tex) uses both components of the abscissa sum and three complete detectors. Its fixed sufficient parameter is `N=15`; the input-preserving normalization has exact height `149^30` and canonical expanded size 74,007 bytes. The current certified inequality fails at $N=14$; this is a limitation of that sufficient comparison, not a lower bound for the polynomial family. Sharper constants or a stronger comparison might lower the parameter without changing the variable count. Any algebraic saving must retain every copied guard and prove soundness on each added boundary.

### Complete reference propagation and its height budget

The [three-coordinate reference theorem](../papers/elliptic_charts/three_coordinate_reference_20261115_v40.tex) and the translated-secant chart are fully combined in the current 68-variable construction. Reference units recover both physical detector products without a new inverse coordinate. The complete census is six rational common coordinates including the half-trace, three reference field coordinates, six pair field coordinates including the reciprocals, two product field helpers, two comparison-square field coordinates and two blocks of eighteen rational coordinates.

The [mixed-power height theorem](../papers/elliptic_heights/mixed_power_height_20261116_v45.tex) gives, for $a_{\mathrm{gap}}=B+X^8+T_{\mathrm{sc}}^2$,

```math
h(X)\le\frac{301}{388}h(a_{\mathrm{gap}})+O(1),\qquad
h(T_{\mathrm{sc}})\le\frac{301}{97}h(a_{\mathrm{gap}})+O(1).
```

The quadratic residual $X(m-X)$ and ordinary-abc eight-contact estimate give strict absorption $602/679$. The physical trace satisfies $h(\operatorname{Tr}x(U-V))\le h(T_{\mathrm{sc}})+2h(X)$, so the retained trace-index estimate still closes after absorption. A modified detector that leaves a higher-degree residual must recalculate this budget and include its extra power coordinates. Height absorption alone never supplies a missing rational completeness theorem or quadratic conversion.

The separate [mixed-reference-sign](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/mixed_reference_signs_20261116_v42.tex), [shared-reference-test](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/shared_reference_test_obstructions_20261116_v43.tex), and [duplication-sign](../negative_results/papers/elliptic_charts/duplicated_reference_signs_20261116_v44.tex) theorems remain restrictions on their stated alternative detectors. The complete inverse-error argument in the current source treats exact zero ordinates and mixed torsion branches directly.

## Arithmetic and coefficient obligations

Smaller pole equations need a complete realization theorem. The [conic-chain classification](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/conic_chain_obstruction_20261115_v41.tex) proves that the quadratic family

$$P=p^2,\qquad e^2+bP=1,\qquad D=1+(cb-1)P,\qquad z^2=bD$$

fails on arbitrarily small prepared inputs for **every fixed rational coefficient $c$**. Its four-auxiliary specialization at $c=0$ and five-auxiliary member at $c=1$ already have explicit local obstructions at 29 and 5. This is not a lower bound for other pole models.

Making the first conic automatic also leaves a substantive global problem. The identity $b=(1-e^2)r^2$, $p=1/r$ gives $e^2+bp^2=1$. The [automatic-conic obstruction](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/automatic_conic_obstruction_20261115_v41.tex) proves failure of the remaining congruent-pole equations on dense prepared families, even for the fully split six-contact formula at $e=20/343$. Its noncongruence certificate uses Tunnell's unconditional necessary direction. A viable version of this approach must control the multiplier values actually produced by the detector and prove the remaining rational realization on that restricted set.

A shared pole parameter preserves two separate parity tests. Replacing them by a single rational test is a different proposal and encounters stronger restrictions. The [product-height counterfamilies](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/product_height_obstructions_20261110_v19.tex) show that the product of two contact functions can have trivial odd radical while the scale is large. The [local rational-combination theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/rational_combinations_of_contacts_20261110_v20.tex) excludes every fixed rational parity combination for its specified source family. These are obstructions to the stated implications, not to all single-pole constructions.

Forcing equality of the two contact square classes can prove soundness, but rational completeness becomes a separate problem. At a fixed comparison scale, the [quadratic contact cover](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/quadratic_contact_covers_20261110_v21.tex) has genus four. Allowing both ordered contact arguments to vary gives the [ordered square-class surface](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/ordered_squareclass_surface_20261115_v33.tex), which is of general type. It has neither a dominant rational parametrization nor a fibration with rational or genus-one general fiber. These facts leave open special arithmetic families of witnesses; they exclude the corresponding unrestricted parametrization strategies.

For effective coefficients in the few-variable construction, the unresolved analytic issue is a uniform effective trace-height estimate on the relevant integer-index tuples. The [explicit construction](../papers/few_variables27/explicit27_20261110_v19.tex) uses both rational components and a third pole predicate to avoid that issue. Positivity on selected completeness witnesses cannot replace a height bound needed for every soundness tuple.

For quartic coefficient optimization, invertible rational combinations of quadratic rows preserve the rational zero set, and invertible coordinate rescalings give a bijection of zero sets. Each proposed change must be followed by primitive integer normalization and exact collection of the squared rows. A height claim for a parameter family needs a bound for every allowed parameter, together with a coefficient that attains it; one small test member does not establish that claim. The current [194-coordinate coefficient theorem](../papers/quartic/quartic194_20261117_v73.tex) gives

```math
H(F_N)=\max\{126616122944180358034867798936,\;128\cdot149^{4N}\}
```

in its primitive reference basis, with 2,765 monomials. The equivalent basis with 2,621 monomials has a larger fixed coefficient term; its height agrees for `N ≥ 7`. Neither coefficient crossover specifies a sufficient defining parameter. The exact height is

```math
H(Q_B)=B^2+1
```

for the [68-variable ordinary-abc family](../papers/quartic_existence/quartic_abc68_20261117_v65.tex). Exact expansion proves these height formulas for the stated parameter ranges. A sufficient defining parameter in each ineffective family still depends on the explicitly retained arithmetic premises.

The [lower-bound results](../negative_results/papers/lower_bounds/lower_bounds_20261109_v15.tex) require degree at least four for a single defining equation and at least five total coordinates for a globally nonnegative quartic or a quadratic system. They supply no lower bound near the current counts.

## Rings of integers of number fields

No counterexample to $\mathcal O_K$ being Diophantine in its fraction field $K$ has been established. The [number-field reductions](../papers/number_fields/number_field_definability_20261109_v12.tex) give three useful formulations:

1. $\mathcal O_K$ is Diophantine in $K$ if and only if $\mathbb Z$ is Diophantine in $K$, using the cited theorem defining $\mathbb Z$ inside $\mathcal O_K$.
2. Assuming a definition of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathbb Q$, these are equivalent to the rational subfield $\mathbb Q$ being Diophantine in $K$.
3. A counterexample persists through every finite extension. Hence one could pass to a totally imaginary Galois extension when searching for a counterexample.

A failure after extending the witnesses of a particular polynomial is a failure of that polynomial. It does not establish the third assertion's counterexample hypothesis. The separate [unchanged-formula obstruction](../negative_results/papers/number_fields/unchanged_formula_obstruction_20261116_v42.tex) proves that no fixed finite collection of formulas can work unchanged throughout all finite extensions; field-dependent constructions remain possible.

The specified $j=1728$ elliptic curve cannot be transferred unchanged across adjoining $i$. The [complex-multiplication theorem](../negative_results/papers/number_fields/complex_multiplication_and_trace_cancellation_20261110_v19.tex) proves exact rank doubling and constructs an infinite cyclic subgroup with zero abscissa trace and unbounded heights. It also supplies distinct doubled points with zero abscissa sum. A number-field construction must use another curve or prove a height statement that controls these cancellation directions.

Rank stability is useful but does not itself define the rational subfield: it supplies an infinite Diophantine subset of rational abscissas, which can remain arithmetically sparse. Likewise, writing a norm or trace in base-field coordinates does not force unrestricted extension-field variables to lie in the base field. The [norm-and-trace equivalences](../papers/number_fields/number_field_definability_20261109_v12.tex), Section 4, identify that missing restriction explicitly.

One sufficient positive target is a Diophantine set $T\subset K$ containing every rational integer and having uniformly bounded integral denominators. Indeed, for an integral basis $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_n$, the set $S=\sum_i\omega_iT$ contains $\mathcal O_K$ and satisfies $MS\subseteq\mathcal O_K$ for some positive integer $M$. A finite union of translates of $MS$ by representatives of $\mathcal O_K/M\mathcal O_K$ then equals $\mathcal O_K$. The [finite-correspondence and denominator theorems](../negative_results/papers/number_fields/finite_correspondences_and_denominators_20261109_v14.tex) exclude several routes to such a set, including quasi-finite covers of abelian varieties and their stated low-genus extensions. A successful construction must avoid those hypotheses or use exceptional positive-dimensional fibers with a proved arithmetic completeness argument.

The decisive goals are therefore a complete shared-parameter pole realization, a globally quadratic propagation of the three-coordinate chart, an exact integral carrier for the Pell compression, or a new number-field denominator mechanism. Each would change an actual arithmetic dependency rather than only the presentation of an existing formula.

## Further coefficient uniformity for the coupling obstruction

A separate research target is to replace the obstructed coefficient progression by all sufficiently large shifts. The following route has not received a complete independent proof audit and is not used by any result record. For fixed $e$, put $m=57^e-4^{e+1}$ and choose a root $y$ of $Y^e+m$ whose field avoids the three quadratic subfields of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$. Study the quadratic extension obtained from $\sqrt{y/4-B}$. If it acquired one of those subfields, a corresponding field norm would force a rational square value of a fixed minimal polynomial at $4B$. For even degree, the monic polynomial and its polynomial square-root expansion suggest an elementary large-$B$ exclusion. Odd-degree factors would require a separate integral-point finiteness argument. The field intersections and uniform exceptions must be proved before using this route.

If the resulting root field is disjoint from $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$, Chebotarev could supply contact primes $p\equiv3\pmod8$. At those primes the relevant $j=1728$ reduction has order $(p\pm1)^2$, allowing the input congruence and the reduction-index congruence to be synchronized. A complete finite-menu proof must also choose the primes with no cross-divisibility between the primes and these reduction orders, preserve the ten other nonsquare branches, and certify valuation-one lifts. These compatibility steps remain an audit target; no stronger all-shift obstruction is asserted here.
