The ordinary-abc quartic is reduced from **59 to 58 total variables**, including the input. Its **50 quadratic residuals** have a sum of squares with **867 coordinate monomials**, **870 parameter-separated terms**, exact degree four and content one. For every positive integer `B`, the exact coefficient height is

```math
H(F_B)=\max\{22838305425411,\ B^2+4091\}.
```

Every sufficiently large fixed odd `B` gives rational input projection equal to the integers under ordinary rational abc and the named arithmetic inputs. The sufficient threshold remains ineffective. The [complete proof](../papers/quartic_existence/one_helper58_20261119_v80.tex), [expanded coefficients](../computation/variable_reductions_v80/abc/quartic58/polynomial.json), [quadratic equations](../computation/variable_reductions_v80/abc/quartic58/quadrics.json), and [independent checker](../computation/variable_reductions_v80/abc/check58.py) are provided. The translated-secant chart and shared Laurent compilation originate in **notes of Gabe Conant with Astra 6.2**. The [v78 source audit](results_20261119_v78.md), [v79 derivative construction](results_20261119_v79.md) and present continuation distinguish those contributions from later project work.

## One normalization coordinate replaces two

Retain the v79 derivative contact `u`, its inverse `c`, the shared detector `W`, and the rational selector

```math
D\operatorname{Tr}(W)=\operatorname{Nm}(W-1).
```

Write `D=c d_s`. Introduce just one new scalar `alpha` and use

```math
\alpha=u+d_s^2=u+u^2D^2,\qquad
\tau(1+\alpha)=\alpha,\qquad b=\frac{7\tau^2}{u}.
```

The stored pole input is `b_s=7 tau²`, because its physical value is `c b_s`. These are three quadratic residuals. They replace the four v79 normalization and pole-input residuals, deleting one coordinate and one equation. The other **47 residuals are unchanged**.

The denominator `1+alpha` has an exact polynomial guard: `alpha=−1` would make the middle equation read `0=−1`. No inverse coordinate is needed. The zero `alpha=0` is harmless in the algebraic specification and is excluded in the defining range by the positive odd contact radical.

For a prime outside the fixed exceptions, put `mu=v_p(u)`, `d=v_p(D)`, and assume `mu` is odd. The two summands of `alpha` have valuations of opposite parity, so

```math
v_p(\alpha)=\min\{\mu,2\mu+2d\}.
```

If `mu<0`, then `alpha` has negative valuation, `tau` is a unit, and `v_p(b)=−mu>0`. This handles arbitrary actual elliptic-point denominators. If `mu>0`, the native pole obstruction rules out `v_p(alpha)≤0`; it then forces `v_p(alpha)>mu/2`, giving

```math
v_p(D)>-3\mu/4.
```

This is precisely the inequality required by the unchanged four-test extraction and height argument. The strict height margin remains `92221/99700`. At real infinity, `u>0` gives `alpha≥u>0`, hence `0<tau<1` and `0<b<7/u`. The integer-completeness proof includes the selected primes, all other prime divisors, two-adic preparation, the fixed prime seven and the zero-input fiber.

## Using the published pole theorem in its stated square class

The fresh primary-source audit checked [Burungale, arXiv:2611.16130v1, Theorem B](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16130v1). Its first squarefree parameter is in the class **seven modulo eight**. The v78 small-input lemma, inherited in v79, explicitly retained a separate square-class-one twist premise. The new factor seven matches the published theorem directly.

For the constructed native input, `0<b<1`, the reduced denominator is a square, `b` is in `7(Q₂×)²`, and `v₂(b)≥4`. Write

```math
b=\beta z^2,\qquad 1-b=\eta d^2
```

with positive squarefree `beta,eta`. They are coprime, `beta≡7 mod 8`, `eta≡1 mod 8`, and each is a local square at every prime dividing the other. Thus they meet the exact hypotheses of Theorem B, including the case `eta=1`. The resulting pair of congruent-number curves supplies the native pole witnesses through explicit rational formulas, whose identities are independently checked. Their ordinates are nonzero because the required positive abscissa square class differs from the positive two-torsion abscissa classes.

This removes the additional square-class-one premise from the new construction. It does not constitute a full independent proof of Burungale's theorem or of the other retained arithmetic inputs.

## Fresh audit of the 59-variable result

The v79 builder and independent reconstruction were rerun without changing any of their coefficient files. The new mathematical review checked the two signs of odd valuation, native zero-coordinate cases, derivative zero slots, automatic guards, uniformity of the height constants and the order of integer-witness preparation.

Two essential external statements were checked in their primary sources:

- [Rudnick–Sarnak–Zaharescu, Appendix A.2, equation (A.2)](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/rsz.pdf) states the homogeneous squarefree-form radical consequence of ordinary rational abc. The fixed forms in the derivative proof satisfy its hypotheses.
- [Sun, arXiv:2610.20691v3, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20691v3) permits any fixed finite list of distinct integral coefficients, prescribed denominator divisibility, lower bounds, and the stated Gaussian-order conditions. Clearing the fixed rational contact denominators gives the sixteen coefficients required here; uniformity in those coefficients is not needed.

The v79 theorem remains valid under its explicitly retained premises. The present construction improves both the variable count and the pole-premise requirement. Original mathematical review hashes and all historical coefficient checks are preserved.

One tempting alternative contact quotient was rejected exactly: replacing `u` by `F³/F_a²` loses the radical estimate on the allowed diagonal `x=y=a`. There `F=G(a)²`, making the new quotient a rational square whenever defined. It therefore has no positive odd prime support. No result is inferred from that rejected replacement.

## Other routes and next steps

The counts **25**, **165**, **180**, and **11** have not been reduced in this continuation. The general contact merger still needs a sufficiently strong bound for lost odd prime support. The general quartics need a smaller scalar point chart or a new exact sharing of quadratic products. The unrestricted-degree conditional route needs rational realization after removing a pole parameter, or an alternative index-recovery construction with fewer point coordinates.

For that last route, the [intrinsic pole equation](../papers/pole_parameters/intrinsic_pole_20261117_v56.tex) provides a concrete target. Its radicand is

```math
P_d(v,s)=d(d+v^2)^2s^4-d(d-v^2)^2(1-d)^2.
```

If `s²=1+d`, then `P_d(0,s)=4d⁴`; the pole witnesses are explicit on this locus. The actual eleven-variable detector input is `e=7u[Nm(W)/((1+u)W₀)]²`. Thus the constructive question is whether its same point tuple and contact slope can also satisfy

```math
Y^2=((1+u)W_0)^2+7u\operatorname{Nm}(W)^2
```

with all original guards and integer-witness preparations. This is an unresolved rational-realization problem. The prepared-input theorem alone does not give the square: `d=112/841=7(4/29)²` is prepared, while `1+d=953/841` is nonsquare because `30²<953<31²`.

Nor does automatic calibration settle the problem. The substitution

```math
d=\frac{28uD^2}{(1-7uD^2)^2},\qquad
1+d=\left(\frac{1+7uD^2}{1-7uD^2}\right)^2
```

has the desired square identically, but loses extraction: for every odd prime other than seven, `v_p(u)=1` and `v_p(D)=−2` give `v_p(d)=3`, without the required detector bound `v_p(D)>−3/4`. Indeed the numerator has valuation `−3` and the squared denominator `−6`. A successful construction must solve the square condition at the actual source tuple while retaining the detector implication. Density that permits avoiding finitely many equations does not supply this additional equality.

For the 58-variable quartic, the next question is whether the new scalar can be combined with a retained coordinate without losing a zero fiber or introducing an uncontrolled denominator. Every further deletion must preserve both soundness and integer completeness.
