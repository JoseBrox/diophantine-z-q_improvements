## A trace-only criterion for 21/9

The [trace-conic theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/trace_conic_criterion_20261117_v57.tex) uses the three-term radicand

$$
\mathcal C=V^2+w(2-V),\qquad V=\operatorname{Tr}(W^{-1}).
$$

At a hypothetical negative odd pole of $b=4w/V^2$, the positive odd valuation of $w$ is smaller than $2v_p(V)$ and $2-V$ is a unit. Thus $\mathcal C$ has odd valuation and cannot be a rational square. At every prepared character, $w>0$ and $0<V\le2$, making the radicand strictly positive. All original guards and the complete **21/9** census are retained under the explicit actual-trace realization hypothesis.

The finite menu $\{1,1+24V,1+3(V-1)^2\}$ may multiply the radicand. Its classes preserve all bad-pole parities, so they require no new height budget, including for the general source. The linear-class radicand has degree three, five monomials and coefficient height 47 in $(V,w)$; these are block measurements, not complete determinant bounds. Its Weierstrass model is

$$
Y_c^2=X_c^3-(2+24w)X_c^2+(1+1176w)X_c,
\qquad(X_c,Y_c)=(1+24V,24Y),
$$

and has the reference point $(49,336)$. A broader admissible family is

$$
q=2+wc^2,\qquad\delta_c=(q^2-2q-1)^2+4q(q^2-1)V,
$$

where $c$ is a fixed guarded rational scalar function of the retained coordinates. Its values at $V=0,1,2$ are the squares $(q^2-2q-1)^2$, $(q^2+1)^2$ and $(q^2+2q-1)^2$. A valuation argument also covers poles of $c$, so no support-height bound is needed. The choice $c=\lambda$ retains the source's ordered fixed-place preparation. All whole-character positivity and denominator guards are included; $c$ is not a new free coordinate.

Both ordinary conic parametrizations and the whole-character proof are explicit. The required **simultaneous rational points at actual detector traces remain unproved**.

## Positive rank throughout the normalized range

The [cubic rank theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/cubic_trace_rank_20261117_v57.tex) determines every nonsingular rational weight for which $(49,336)$ is torsion: $296/49$ and $-96/49$ give order three, and $100/49$ gives order four. Consequently the section has infinite order for every rational $0<w\le1/4$.

Mazur's torsion classification reduces the proof to multiples 8, 10 and 12. Exact polynomial identities and projective residue certificates rule out all other rational zeros of their division polynomials. A standard-library verifier checks the complete coefficient arrays, and a separate tangent-and-chord computation checks the multiplication denominators. The [reproducible package](../computation/pole_parameters/cubic_trace_rank_v57) contains both audits.

The real curve is connected in this range. Multiples in a suitable arithmetic progression are therefore dense over the reals while returning to any chosen finite-place neighborhoods of the reference point. This gives infinitely many rational free traces in $(1,2)$ and permits finitely many nonzero guards to be avoided. It does not prescribe the actual detector trace, so it does not close the 21/9 completeness gap.

## Square classes with a height budget

The [variable-class theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/variable_squareclasses_20261117_v57.tex) allows each native radicand to belong to a fixed exceptional-unit multiple of a class selected from

$$
\{1,\ (X^2+B)^2+\operatorname{Tr}(x(A))^2,\ 1+X^4\}.
$$

The selected class may have prime support outside the exceptional set. Its odd support costs at most $2h(1:X^2+B:\operatorname{Tr}(x(A)))+O(1)$; stronger height amplification absorbs this cost. A finite product of the guarded branch encoders adds no coordinate.

For two covers, the ordinary-abc choice is $(\kappa,e)=(36/35,6)$, with strict contraction $135/136$. For one cover it is $(\kappa,e,d)=(251/250,14,8)$, with strict contraction $103006635/103133368$. The single-cover height argument has total degree **224**, minimized within the stated even-power criterion. It is not the degree of a complete defining polynomial.

These are soundness and census theorems. A **nine-variable definition still requires actual rational source points and a common slope or square parameter realizing the allowed classes for every integer input**. The theorem neither proves that realization nor transfers the variable classes to the unrestricted 21-variable target.

## Separate obstruction results

The [specialization theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/intrinsic_specializations_20261117_v57.tex) excludes every finite menu of rational functions $s=R_j(d)$ from uniform completeness on prepared inputs. It also identifies the positive abscissa classes on the relevant rank-one congruent curves. Relations coupling $s$ to the other coordinate, higher rank, and restricted actual detector families remain outside its scope.

The [prepared-cover obstruction](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/prepared_sha_obstruction_20261117_v57.tex) gives the prepared input $d=112/225$. The curve

$$
y^2=d((1-d)^2-s^4)
$$

has a point over every completion of $\mathbb Q$ and has no rational point. The exact full descent and Cassels pairing on $E_{791}$ give rank one and abscissa image $\{[1],[-1],[791],[-791]\}$, excluding the required class $[-7]$. Two independent exact checkers verify the matrix, tangent identities, local symbol and point data; the manuscript invokes the cited Monsky and Cassels theorems explicitly. This is an obstruction to the specified one-coordinate specialization on prepared inputs, not a claim about an actual detector tuple.
