## Exact preservation of the detector

Write

```math
W=\prod_k(1+w h_k^2),\qquad
V=\operatorname{Tr}(W^{-1}),\qquad J=\operatorname{Norm}(W^{-1}),
\qquad b=4w/V^2.
```

The transformation

```math
w^*=w/(1+w)^2,\qquad W^*=(1+w)W,\qquad
V^*=V/(1+w),\qquad J^*=J/(1+w)^2
```

leaves `b` exactly unchanged. The new product still has the native form: multiply every old `h_k` by `1+w` and add the factor with `h_0^*=1+w`. The guard `1+w≠0` is included and holds automatically on prepared tuples, where `w>0`.

Every odd valuation of `w*` is positive. At a hypothetical negative odd valuation of `b` outside the original exceptional set, the native residue alternatives therefore force

```math
(w^*,V^*,J^*)\equiv(0,0,0).
```

This permits the level multiplier

```math
m=\frac{P(V^*,J^*)}{Q(V^*,J^*)},\qquad a=17m^2,
```

when both polynomials have coefficients integral outside the original exceptional set and `Q(0,0)` is a unit there. The numerator and denominator remain guarded. Their nonvanishing at prepared characters is a separate condition; it is proved for the concrete choices in the manuscript.

For example,

```math
m=\frac1{1-V^*+J^*},\qquad
1-V^*+J^*=\operatorname{Norm}(1-(W^*)^{-1})>0
```

at every prepared character. This multiplier is integral at every hypothetical bad pole but may have a pole at a good contact. An exact rational native-product example in the certificate has multiplier valuation minus one at 7. It is an algebraic local example, not an actual elliptic completeness witness.


