# Elliptic index divisibility and the limits of quotient-height detectors

The current complete construction counts are **25** under the retained general premises, **13** under either stated weak-abc hypothesis, and **11** under ordinary rational abc. The ordinary-abc quartic has **71** variables. Every count includes the input. **Neither 21 nor 9 is established.** The complete eleven-variable circuit and its degree bound of 5,444,199,553 are unchanged.

## Primitive denominator primes

Fix a congruent-number curve over the rationals and a non-torsion point in its fourfold subgroup. If `H=[h]P` and `q_H^2=x(H)`, duplication and weak abc at exponent $\kappa$ give

```math
\log L_{h,S}\ge\left(\frac2\kappa-1\right)h(q_H)-O(1),
```

where $L_{h,S}$ is the product of denominator primes of valuation exactly one outside a fixed finite set. Under weak abc at $4/3$, subtraction of the nonprimitive support leaves a positive quadratic-height term. Thus every sufficiently large index has a simple primitive denominator prime.

For any fixed $1<\kappa<2$, choose an integer $R>1/(2/\kappa-1)$ and let $N$ be the product of the primes at most $R$. On indices coprime to $N$, the old support costs at most $h(q_H)/R+O(\log|h|)$. This proves the same primitive-prime conclusion. The progression $h\equiv1\pmod N$ is compatible with the later completeness construction.

Proof: [simple primitive denominators](../papers/elliptic_divisibility/simple_primitive_denominators_20261117_v60.tex). Exact arithmetic: [checker and certificate](../computation/elliptic_divisibility/primitive_denominators_v60).

## A complete index-divisibility subsource

For $H=[h]P$ and $U=[j]P$, a balanced intrinsic-pole test forces $h\mid j$. Its proof includes the fixed subgroup and index-coset conditions, exceptional-index guards, real preparation, the places two and seven, and all geometric-character guards. The finite isogeny covers are given by monic algebras; their preimage ordinates are recovered from the differential of the multiplication map.

For every nonzero integer $m$, there are infinitely many prepared choices with $j=mh$. Formal-logarithm truncation amplifies the comparison with $X$ to any fixed order $T$: every simple reference-denominator prime outside the fixed exceptional set divides $X-m$ to order at least $T$. The required odd weight exponent is $8T-7$, and no new free coordinate is added.

If $X\ne m$, the resulting inequality is

```math
T\left(\frac2\kappa-1\right)h(q_H)
\le h(X)+\log(1+|m|)+O_T(1).
```

This identifies a missing growth condition; it does not supply it. In particular, a real size condition does not control rational height. Proof and full scope: [elliptic index poles](../papers/elliptic_divisibility/elliptic_index_poles_20261117_v60.tex). Exact checks: [index-pole certificate](../computation/elliptic_divisibility/index_poles_v60).

## Pole cancellation and a decisive support obstruction

The coordinate $Y=X^2x(U)-x(H)$ satisfies a uniform height inequality. On $X=m$ and $U=[m]H$, its pole at $H=O$ cancels, with leading term

```math
Y=\frac{d^2(m^4-1)}5\,\zeta(H)^2+O(\zeta(H)^6).
```

These positive statements are proved in the separate [quotient-height paper](../papers/elliptic_heights/pole_cancelling_quotient_height_20261117_v60.tex).

Using this coordinate in the specified two-point derivative detector cannot give completeness. That detector forces its entire positive odd contact support into the denominator of $H$, so its logarithmic radical is at most $\widehat h(H)+O(1)$. The radical and coordinate lower bounds instead give

```math
\left(\frac{187845767}{200600400}(m^2-1)-1\right)
\widehat h(H)\le O(1).
```

Thus $m$ is bounded for every fixed source. With the other data fixed, sufficiently large coefficient shift $B$ excludes every intended input $|m|\ge2$. This is a proved obstruction to that detector design, not an unresolved local search problem or a lower bound for all nine-variable definitions. Its [proof](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/denominator_support_height_obstruction_20261117_v60.tex) is in `negative_results`.

The obstruction also covers every fixed rational replacement `R(X,H,U)` that is generically nonconstant in `U`, under the same support and height inequalities. A uniform rational-root height bound controls nonconstant specializations. On the degeneration locus, either the input is constant, the reference point is constant and its height bounds the input, or a positive-genus curve maps nontrivially to both factors. Siegel’s theorem then gives only finitely many intended integer inputs. No nondegeneracy guard is required for that finiteness conclusion. This statement concerns `X=m`, `U=[m]H`; it does not assert finiteness of all rational inputs of an unidentified source.

A separate [perturbation theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/square_rescaled_weight_perturbation_20261117_v60.tex) proves that square-rescaling a weight whose square class is independent of $X$ preserves prepared witnesses near noninteger inputs. Such rescalings cannot identify the external input with the integer index quotient.

## Remaining arithmetic targets
