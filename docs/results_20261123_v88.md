# Bhargava–Burungale v2: audit, stronger interfaces and lower degrees

**Date:** 23 November 2026. **Source:** Manjul Bhargava and Ashay Burungale, [A Diophantine definition of Z in Q, arXiv:2611.16130v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16130v2). **Baseline:** commit 75bdd2161287a9a7bee64e86865402167ad36ce2 of this repository.

This continuation audits the paper section by section, checks its decisive external theorem interfaces, proves several extensions, and integrates a normalized product pole into all five current few-variable routes. The complete defining-variable records remain **25, 27 and 11**, and the principal quartics remain **163, 179 and 56**. All five unrestricted-degree bounds improve. The paper's own construction admits smaller arithmetic blocks and one fewer rational variable in its interval wrapper, but its previously unspecified parameter \(r\) is still unspecified. These symbolic savings are not new numerical records.

The definitive numbers, source paths and qualifications are in [the v88 results ledger](../data/results_20261123_v88.json). The [all-route table](bb_all_route_status_20261123_v88.md) also covers weaker-abc quartics, general fixed-exponent families, number-field constructions and active smaller-variable criteria. No global optimality claim is made.

## 2. Product poles and all five degree improvements

For the actual input \(e=dk^2\), with \(d,k\ne0\) already represented by the circuit, use

\[
Y^2=(1-e)(v^4-d^2)(s^4-d^2).
\]

The substitution \((s_0,t_0,z_0)=(kv,ks,k^4Y)\) recovers BB Lemma 2.2 exactly. The new radicand is linear in the complicated detector input; the old radicand was quadratic in \(1-e\). Square extraction supplies \(d,k\) without another free coordinate. This explains the degree saving and the unchanged variable count.

The selected representative matters to the exact ledger. The package records square-factor redistributions and the finite normalization menus tested. They establish the best bounds in those menus, not global minimum degrees. Every normalization retains \(e=dk^2\), all denominators and every final guard.

For the two general sources set

\[
\rho_*=102^2\,67^L\frac{7q}{(1+7q)^2},\quad
q=\frac{(1224(67^{(L+1)/2}+1))^2\beta(\beta-1)}
{(\beta-2401)(32\beta+343)},\quad \beta=1632X^2+2477.
\]

This has the required 2-adic class seven and even depth at least ten, square denominator, \(v_{67}(\rho_*)=L\), and \(0<\rho_*<1/167\). Consumed-prime parity and depth are preserved outside the existing exceptional set, which already contains seven. The sharper estimate

\[
h(\rho_*)\le4h(\beta)+2L\log67+66\log2
\]

implies the old additive bound 128. Therefore **the 27-variable threshold \(L=143\) remains certified** under its retained premises. The same value is only a fixture for the ineffective 25-variable family. The old 17-adic square condition belonged to the replaced native-pole converse; reference-continuity and depth conditions remain satisfied.

The ordinary-abc source already has prepared class seven. The weaker-abc sources keep their weight \(b\) and use \(e=7b\), preparing seven before prime selection. The old asymptotic \(bz^8\to1\) gives nonnegative even \(v_7(b)\); the real neighborhood is tightened to \(0<b<1/7\). The height margins and ordered construction are unchanged. The two weaker-abc degree bounds become **16,460,929** and **9,808,001** at exponents \(121/100\) and \(16/15\). A finite search of 48 admissible parameter/normalization choices retained the selected two winners.

**Terminal zero roots must be allowed.** A nonselected character can have \(e=1\). The final positive encoder explicitly removes the inherited root guard, and the checker proves that no retained denominator depends on that root. The weak-abc conic denominator disappears, so its staged guard count is rebuilt. All five sources, not merely their final numerical receipts, are supplied.

## 3. Simultaneous twists with overlapping supports

Fix squarefree \(j\equiv7\bmod8\), with prime support \(J\). Let \(K\) be a finite set of odd primes outside \(J\), with \((j/q)=1\) for \(q\in K\). For every divisor \(k\mid\prod_{q\in K}q\) satisfying

\[
k\equiv1\pmod8,\qquad (k/p)=1\quad(p\in J),
\]

the same squarefree \(n\equiv5\bmod8\), divisible by \(j\) and prime to \(K\), works: \(E_{nk}\) has analytic and algebraic rank one, finite odd-order \(\Sha\), and a point in abscissa class \(j\mathbb Q^{\times2}\). There are infinitely many such \(n\), with \(2|J|+|K|\) prime factors and arbitrary fixed finite avoidance for the added primes.

Complements may overlap arbitrarily. The proof keeps all subdivision primes while allowing complement leaves to be absent, and checks the reduced descent and genus calculation in every fiber. The exact checker validates 289 formal graph fibers, ten concrete curves and eleven independently enumerated class-number comparisons. It does not certify analytic ranks numerically; the infinite theorem follows from the proof and TYZ.

For prepared native poles with a common input squareclass and \(h\) distinct nontrivial complement classes, **\(h+1\) retained free pole parameters** suffice, with finite quadratic choices. If all complements are squares, a special zero-root solution needs no free pole parameter. Uniform finite-choice equations and possible zero denominators at other characters are recorded. This does not automatically subtract variables: the actual common-class condition, rationality of the finite choices, all-character guards and encoder transfer must still be established.

A rank-one limitation is equally precise. For \(n\equiv5\bmod8\), the first Kummer image is generated by \(-1,n,j\). Its positive classes have residues \(1,5,7,3\bmod8\), so exactly one has residue seven. Different class-seven detector inputs cannot share that same rank-one curve by this construction.

The companion equation

\[
(s^2+c)(t^2-c)=(1-c)r^2(s^2-c)(t^2+c)
\]

has degree seven, twelve monomials and coefficient height one, with the same soundness and prepared converse. It still has three witnesses. Its nonmonic last variable prevents an automatic improvement of the current monic tower.

## 4. Shorter arithmetic in the BB index model

For \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{13})\), \(d=(1+\sqrt{13})/2\), \(E:y^2=x^3-d^2x\), and \(\Gamma=2E(F)=\mathbb ZP_0\), set \(x^*(O)=0\) by a finite disjunction. Transferring the project's weighted aggregate-trace method gives

\[
n_1^2+n_2^2\le A h\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}
(x^*(n_1P_0)+x^*(n_2P_0))\right)+J.
\]

The proof checks von Buhren's projective-normality hypothesis, a fixed finite-rank subgroup, noncancellation of poles on abelian translates, independence of the conjugate CM twists over \(\mathbb Z[i]\), and all zero-index patterns. The aggregate-trace idea is prior project work; its transfer and use here are new.

On a nonzero stratum the trace \(T>\sqrt{13}>3\), so \(J\) is absorbed into \(A_*h(T)\). Two predicates at \(s\) and \(s+T^k\), with fixed sufficiently large \(k\), force \(n_1^2=n_2\). Since \(h(T^k)=kh(T)\), no large elliptic multiplication map is required. This uses **36 Gaussian auxiliary points instead of 90** per squaring relation. They are points over an auxiliary field, not rational-variable counts.

Every fixed integral quadratic relation \(F_0(n_1,\ldots,n_s)=0\) also has a direct two-test definition. Including \(P_0\) in the trace gives

\[
1+\sum n_j^2\le A_{*,s}h(T),\qquad
|F_0(\mathbf n)|\le C_{F_0}(1+\sum n_j^2).
\]

Each local predicate has six tuples of \(s+1\) Gaussian points and \(3(s+1)\) local factors per tuple. Formal logarithms give \(M(\xi)^{24}\mid F_0(\mathbf n)\); Frobenius supplies witnesses when it vanishes. Two tests suffice for \(k>A_{*,s}(2HC_{F_0}+\log2)\). The result is restricted to degree at most two.

For multiplication, \(|n_1n_2-n_3|\le(1+n_1^2+n_2^2+n_3^2)/2\), certified by two sums of squares. Thus **48 Gaussian auxiliary points** suffice, versus 108 through three improved squarings or 270 through three original ones. The complete rational-coordinate realization still needs compilation.

The Green–Tao lemma extends to arbitrary finite rational shift and root sets, and any positive-relative-upper-density prime set. Choosing sufficiently large progression indices prevents cancellation with the common denominator. This is enough for the two-shift construction. It does not reproduce the different square-denominator, Gaussian ray-class, opposite-order, synchronized-index and real-approximation requirements of the original Sun-based source without further proof.

## 5. One fewer rational variable in the interval wrapper

For \(Q=(d+1,d+2)\), the exact double \(P_*=2Q\in\Gamma\) has

\[
x(P_*)=(187+19\sqrt{13})/72=a+b\sqrt{13},
\qquad v_2(a)=-2,\quad v_2(b)=-3.
\]

A small finite-index coset \(P_*+[N]\Gamma\) preserves these valuations and the required real density. For integral \(q\), choose \(a<q<b\) with arbitrarily small width. Then \((q-a)(b-q)>0\) has 2-adic valuation \(-5\), so Legendre–Gauss represents it by three rational squares.

The definition uses exactly the original index set \(T\); the coset is only a completeness choice. No new variable or coset constraint is added, and soundness follows from nonnegativity. Relative to the same quadratic presentation of \(T\), the wrapper drops from **\(r+19\) to \(r+18\) auxiliaries**, hence **\(r+20\) to \(r+19\) total variables**. Neither \(r\) nor the sufficient amplification power is evaluated.

## 6. Quartics, other fields and unresolved smaller targets

The product pole is economical in unrestricted degree, but does not automatically have a cheaper quadratic circuit. An attempted deletion of the shared \(R^2\) register was audited in the full two-unit Laurent model. Six physical rows force incompatible inequalities for all real diagonal reweightings of variables and rows. Nineteen-term integer Farkas certificates give \(0\le-9\) and \(0\le-6\) for the 163 and 179 systems. The [negative theorem](../negative_results/R_square_reweighting_v88/R_square_obstruction_20261123_v88.tex) excludes neither nonlinear row combinations nor different charts and proves no global lower bound.

The weaker-abc quartics retain 63, 64 and 67 variables with their exact exponent hypotheses; the \(61+r\) family retains its strict height inequality. The number-field selector still uses ten \(K\)-coordinates, with compositions 110 for \(\mathbb Z\) and 220 for the ring of integers of a quadratic field under their hypotheses. A lower inner polynomial degree does not reduce the number of rationality constraints.

The **21/9 targets are neither proved nor disproved**. The missing step is a rational point at the actual coupled detector value, with every physical lift and denominator condition. Prescribed classes on independently chosen twists do not prove that additional equality. Neither local solubility, finite successful searches, auxiliary-cover points nor a symbolic parameter-sharing count settles it. Prepared-fiber counterexamples remain scoped to their own families and are not asserted to occur at the actual tuple.
