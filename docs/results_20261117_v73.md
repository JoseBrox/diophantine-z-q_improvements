## Arithmetic construction

Four cross products replace alternate formal-parameter ratios. The input test is the numerator `N = S − beta*s_1`. A homogeneous Lucas circuit recovers the third and fourth primary ratios only where the local proof needs them.

At every consumed prime, the factor valuations have distinct residue classes, so the pole obstruction forces all required reciprocal and comparison tests to be positive at one conjugate valuation slot. The reciprocal tests then make the local ratio denominators nonzero. The recovered comparisons have depth at least three, the Lucas residual has depth at least three, and the input comparison has depth at least two. The global proof retains the original weaker input divisibility and its established height estimates.

For integer witnesses, the two formal depths must be synchronized. The retained prime-pattern separation allows extra prime-power congruences on the reference index without changing its annihilation, fixed-place or real conditions. Raising only the smaller depth gives equal depths at each selected contact. This is enough for the actual factors and actual pole inputs at their selected contact depth one. The argument does not assume that both adjacent reference indices can be made arbitrarily divisible by a prime.

The proof includes zero numerator ordinates and finite two-torsion differences. A vanishing denominator precludes a consumed prime; the resulting radical-one case is covered by the unchanged height argument. The scalar difference remains nontorsion through its retained primary and scalar quotient equations.

The six literal unit equations force all eight stored contact units to be nonzero before any Laurent inverse is taken. Thus the compiled system has an exact input-preserving inverse to the physical source. The two constant reference bases have the same rational zero set, and a sum of rational squares vanishes precisely when all its rows vanish.
