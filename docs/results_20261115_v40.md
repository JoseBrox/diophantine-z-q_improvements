# Exact degree and coefficient improvements

The current constructions are summarized in [the README](../README.md) and the [machine-readable results](../data/results_20261115_v40.json). Every count includes the input. The priorities are the number of variables, degree, and coefficient height. The variable counts are unchanged: **25 and 13** for the few-variable constructions, **196 and 78** for the corresponding quartics, and **27 and 258** for the fully specified inherited-premise members.

The coefficient height of the explicit 27-variable member is below **2^(2^70)**. The other sufficient defining parameters remain ineffective. The stored small-parameter members are reconstruction fixtures. These figures are upper bounds for finite determinant specifications, not claimed exact degrees or expanded supports.

Only the final pole radicand may vanish. The separating element still has distinct nonzero values on distinct geometric characters, including when the final quadratic algebra has nilpotents. A rational determinant root selects a unique Galois-fixed character and therefore a rational source solution. All denominator guards and all preceding radicand guards remain. An elementary local valuation argument proves that the final ordinate may be zero without weakening the pole obstruction.

The permitted retained-coordinate locus is larger than the former full-unit locus. Soundness is proved on this larger locus, while the existing full-unit witnesses give completeness. This distinction is part of the theorem; an equality of the old and new loci is not asserted.

The [complete source-bound specifications and checker](../computation/few_variables/terminal_root_v40) reconstruct every denominator and norm-factor condition from the native circuit. Exact verification includes twenty corruption controls and eight small algebras, among them terminal dual-number factors with repeated rational eigenvalues and a counterexample to allowing a nonterminal radicand to vanish.

## Quartic coefficient specifications

The [primitive quadratic basis theorem](../papers/quartic/primitive_quadratic_bases_20261115_v40.tex) supplies the preferred coefficient variants.

| Family | Total variables | Primitive quadratic rows | Quartic monomials |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Inherited-premise quartic | 196 | 176 | 2,994 |
| Ordinary-abc quartic | 78 | 68 | 1,254 |

Their exact heights are

$$H(P_N)=\max\{126616122944180358034867798936,\;32561227748626372080\,149^{2N}\},\qquad N\ge1,$$

and

$$H(Q_B)=\max\{20828275368498373068800,\;B^2+275933278576697172\},\qquad B\ge1.$$

For P_N, the first coefficient bound is attained for N=1,2 and the second for N≥3. This crossover is unrelated to the ineffective defining threshold. For Q_B, the arithmetic construction still requires a sufficiently large fixed odd B≥81. The B² term is retained in the exact formula; reducing the fixed coefficient maximum is not a uniform multiplicative saving in the height when B² dominates.

Both families have degree exactly four and integer content one. The unchanged input-square row contributes coefficient one at X⁴. Each specialized quadratic row remains primitive at every permitted integer parameter. No new prime, pole, rank or height premise is introduced.

The transformations are given by fixed invertible rational matrices, with their inverses certified exactly. The 196-variable family also rescales two contact-combination coordinates by the fixed nonzero constant 1/3813373161796. All rational source solutions correspond bijectively, and the input coordinate is fixed. Reproduction applies the stored matrices directly; it does not require finding a lattice basis or assuming optimality of that basis.

Complete data and independent checkers:

- [196 variables](../computation/quartic/primitive_basis196_v40): source binding, row matrix, coordinate map, primitive-row multipliers, full quadratic and quartic tables, and exact height certificate.
- [78 variables](../computation/quartic/abc78_balanced_v40): source binding, row matrix and inverse, full tables, and all-positive-B height certificate.

The [paired-ratio theorem](../papers/elliptic_charts/paired_ratio_redundancy_20261115_v40.tex) recovers the redundant reciprocal curve equations from the complete ratio and pair equations. Its proof includes identity, torsion, tangent and zero-ratio cases. The corresponding [196-variable application](../papers/quartic/quartic196_redundancy_20261115_v40.tex) removes six rational rows. The alternatives with 2,853 and 1,113 monomials are retained in [paired_redundancy196_v40](../computation/quartic/paired_redundancy196_v40) and [abc78_reduced_v40](../computation/quartic/abc78_reduced_v40). Their larger coefficient heights make them secondary under the stated optimization priorities.

## Auxiliary theorems and their limits

The [three-coordinate elliptic chart](../papers/elliptic_charts/three_coordinate_reference_20261115_v40.tex) gives two quadrics in three field coordinates and an exact isomorphism with the elliptic curve minus four specified points. Its inverse maps and unit formulas cover the full domain. Universal identities, exact quadratic-field examples and 11,336 finite-field chart points supplement the proof. Its reciprocal abscissa squares are generally quartic expressions, so the local chart saving is not yet a saving in a complete defining quartic.

The paired-ratio paper also proves a uniform scaling lemma: all seven reciprocal-pair coordinates, including the stored square coordinates, may be scaled by one nonzero field unit while retaining quadratic equations. The surrounding height and detector equations still have to be accounted for before any smaller total can be claimed.

The [Pell-label theorem](../papers/integer_labels/pell_labels_20261115_v40.tex) gives sharp exponential growth using two exact integral labels and a finite quadratic choice. Its polynomial square condition has degree six and coefficient height sixteen. The same paper proves an explicit 19-adic approximation counterexample: exact integral labels cannot be replaced by rational ratios merely close to those labels. Consequently no smaller global count follows without an additional arithmetic argument.

The [research guide](research_directions.md) states the common-parameter pole realization needed for a possible 24-variable construction, the remaining quartic propagation problem, and the number-field denominator problem. These are proof obligations, not new construction records. No number-field counterexample or all-number-fields theorem is established.

## Verification and source scope

Run the current exact checks from the repository root:

```sh
python -B scripts/verify_v40.py --report /tmp/v40-verification.json
```

The verifier reconstructs all new specifications and expanded quartics in temporary files and compares them byte for byte with the distributed tables. It separately executes the independent checkers. The stored [integration receipt](../data/audits/integration_20261115_v40.json) identifies the exact certified files.

The retained arithmetic and coefficient sources are unchanged. The complete repository verification commands are in the README; the legacy suite includes 126 stages and the publication manifest. Optional PARI rank checks are outside that suite unless requested explicitly. All six new TeX papers are compiled and visually inspected; no new PDF is required for reproduction of the mathematics or coefficients.

The mathematical proofs establish the infinite algebraic implications. Finite computations authenticate identities, source bindings and coefficients. Neither is presented as a completed independent audit of every external arithmetic theorem. The [literature references](literature_20261115.md), manuscript bibliographies and source-attribution files retain those distinctions.
