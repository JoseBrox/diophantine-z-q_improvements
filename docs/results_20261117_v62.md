## A height theorem over totally real fields

The separate [effective torsion-sum theorem](../papers/effective_sums/effective_torsion_sums_20261117_v62.tex) applies over a totally real number field $K$ to

```math
E:y^2=x^3-d^2x,\qquad Q_i=[n_i]P+T_i,\qquad
Z=\sum_i x(Q_i)+rd+1,
```

with $d$ totally positive and integral, $P$ of infinite order, and arbitrary torsion translates $T_i$. Every $Q_i$ is finite; its index may be zero or negative. The height loss is explicit and logarithmic in the largest index. The ramification allowance is

```math
R_K=\frac{2}{[K:\mathbb Q]}
\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p,\ p\ne2}
f_{\mathfrak p}\left\lfloor\frac{e_{\mathfrak p}-1}{2}\right\rfloor\log p
\le\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}(K)|}{[K:\mathbb Q]}.
```

It vanishes if every odd ramification index is at most two, including totally real multiquadratic fields. On the normalized source the four-point consequence is $B^2<40h(Z)+2285$. This theorem concerns the algebraic sum, and does not assert the same effective bound for its rational trace. It does not establish the Diophantine definition of every number-field ring of integers.
