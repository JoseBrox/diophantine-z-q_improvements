## How the coauthor reduced the variable counts

### One shared detector for several contact blocks

The decisive general-quartic change is to share a four-factor detector product instead of keeping a separate detector for each contact block. Write `c=Σq_j`, where the `q_j` are reciprocal contact weights. The shifted norm equation

```math
D\operatorname{Tr}(W)=\operatorname{Nm}(W-1)
```

has no zero-denominator rational solution: if the trace were zero, the equation would give `W=1` in the quadratic field, whose trace is two. The contact-specific gate uses `D` and the other reciprocal weights to recover the required valuation at some conjugate slot. The four local valuation classes remain distinct. Signed hyperbolic pairs then allow the products to be compiled using integral Laurent weights and fewer helper coordinates.

The audit checks the shared selector, the physical-to-quadratic maps, synchronization of the elliptic comparison labels, vanishing test values, and the real and local preparation used for integer witnesses. Every published quartic coefficient is independently reconstructed.

The new continuation raises the odd terms in the first three shared factors from `c U²−c³ Z²` to `c⁵ U²−c⁷ Z²`. The old prepared contact depths still suffice, and the valuation classes remain distinct. This strengthens the extracted divisibilities to

```math
M_j^4\mid\Delta,\qquad M_j^3\mid\operatorname{num}(\beta-n)
```

when the latter numerator is nonzero. The improved Laurent table still needs 165 coordinates and 139 quadrics, with one fewer monomial than the supplied table.

### The 62-variable ordinary-abc quartic

The coauthor also shares its two-contact detector and makes an invertible reference-chart change. With the chart outputs `C,I,J,K'`, the field detector is

```math
W=1+cC^4+c^2J^2-c^3\overline I^{4}-c^4\overline{K'}^{2}.
```

Even valuations of the reciprocal-abscissa products make the four negative residue classes distinct modulo four. This supplies extraction even if an individual test or gate vanishes. Tangencies, zero numerator ordinates and finite two-torsion differences remain in the chart. The input zero has a separate checked witness.

Our further improvement removes the constant from the two gate equations:

```math
2\tau_j=q_{1-j}^2+q_jD^2,\qquad q_jb_j=\tau_j^2.
```

The physical pole input now tends to zero at real infinity. A small-input version of the same retained simultaneous-twist premise proves completeness: it suffices that `0<b<1`, that the reduced denominator is a square, and that `b` is a two-adic square of valuation at least four. The proof does not require every odd numerator prime to be `1 mod 4`.

The primitive constant-free presentation has **825 monomials**, and a sparse weighted presentation has **752**. The preferred small-height presentation has **840**, because its coefficient normalization serves a different objective. All have 62 variables, degree four and content one.


The target `τ=X²+1` is always a positive nonzero integer at integer input, and rational `X` with integral `τ` is integral. In the explicit 27-coordinate construction the outer squares on the two varying scales can also be removed. The positive factors preserve local valuation extraction while making the selected rational-character traces and norms nonzero. ### Twelve contacts at the first height scale

For the ordinary-abc eleven-coordinate source put

```math
\beta=X^2+B,\quad T=\operatorname{Tr}x(A),\quad
M=\beta^4+T^4,\quad a=M.
```

Use the twelve shifts

```math

```

and the derivative quotient

```math
F=\prod_c((a+c)^2-z^2),\qquad u=-24F/F_a.
```

The stronger intermediate height estimate in the original derivative argument applies to the first scale. It yields `log r⁺(u)≥(39/40)h(M)−O(1)`. With the original binary-power coefficient `997/1001`, the strict margin is **18863/40040**. No division by `a²−z²` occurs, so its former guard is unnecessary.

The missing two-adic preparation is supplied by a new all-integer trace lemma. For the fixed elliptic generator `P` and every nonzero integer `n`,

```math
n^2\operatorname{Tr}x([n]P)-57/4\in16\mathbb Z_2.
```

Thus the trace has valuation `−2−2v₂(n)` and is itself a two-adic square. The prepared scale `M` is consequently a two-adic square. The proof uses an integral formal group at two, followed by exact duplication; it is not extrapolated from a finite list of multiples.

There is also a reusable intrinsic-pole identity. If `e=dt²` and `t≠0`, the substitutions

```math
v_{\rm old}=tv,\qquad Y_{\rm old}=t^3Y
```

replace the old equation by

```math
Y^2=d(d+v^2)^2s^4-d(d-v^2)^2(1-e)^2.
```

This is a rational bijection, including zero ordinates. For the actual detector one can take `d=7u`. Removing this known square before compiling the finite algebra greatly reduces its degree.

The final guard degree is 650, the separator degree is 49,052 and the algebra rank is 128. Hence

```math
1+128(1+650+49052)=6,361,985.
```

The support is less than `2^212`. The sufficient odd shift is still ineffective. The `B=3` fixture is certified only as a polynomial specification, not as a defining member.

### Automatic elliptic point guards

This simplification applies across all three few-variable routes. In a square-abscissa root chart, `j²=g⁴−D`, where `D=11−6√2`. Its norm radicand cannot vanish at a rational base, since that would force `(Nm g)^4=49`. For a full auxiliary chart, its retained ordinate norm forces the abscissa norm to be nonzero. These facts establish the needed point étaleness before character extraction. After a rational character is extracted, total positivity of `D` excludes `g=0`.

Consequently two root norm atoms and one abscissa norm atom per full auxiliary chart are redundant. Their deletion saves guard degree 14 for eleven coordinates and 50 for the four-root/five-auxiliary constructions. The proof distinguishes integral algebra formation, character extraction and subsequent rational denominator evaluation.

## Weaker hypotheses and generalizations

The supplied 63- and 64-variable quartics work under weaker hypotheses than claimed in their initial tables: `κ=9/8` and `κ=17/14`, respectively, with strict absorption coefficients `18/19` and `68/71`.

For six contacts and gap exponent `2^r`, a sufficient condition is

```math
[2^r-\kappa(2^{r-1}+2)](8/\kappa-6)>4(2\kappa-1),
```

using `59+r` coordinates. Eight contacts replace the height rate by `12/κ−8` and use `61+r` coordinates. Thus for every fixed **`1<κ<3/2`**, taking `r` sufficiently large gives a quartic family under that weak-abc hypothesis and the other retained premises.

The explicit integer contacts are `{1,8,22,28,29,36,43,64}`. They come from the exact degree-six identity

```math
(v+4)(v+20)^3(v-5)^2-(v-4)(v-20)^3(v+5)^2=108v^5
```

under `t=43+210/(v−10)`. The transformation has diameter 63 and avoids zero. The new 63-, 64- and 67-variable integer-contact families all have height `B+1` on sufficiently large odd fourth-power subfamilies. Their numerical coefficient-height cutoffs, respectively 17,207, 17,207 and 192,405, are again separate from their ineffective defining thresholds.

The shared selector also extends to a degree-`d` number field using

```math
D e_{d-1}(W)=\operatorname{Nm}(W-1),
```

where `e_{d-1}` is the penultimate elementary symmetric function. Its denominator cannot vanish at a rational solution. A slot configuration with at least one conjugate near one and all others poles gives positive valuation of `D`, after excluding the finitely many small residue characteristics that can cancel the leading coefficient. This arithmetic extension does not by itself keep the norm equations quadratic.
