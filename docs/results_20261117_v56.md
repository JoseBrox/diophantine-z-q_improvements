## One actual cover suffices for conditional soundness

The [single-cover theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/single_cover_criterion_20261117_v56.tex) retains the three source points $A=[n]P$, $H=[h]P$, $U=[j]P$, every point and difference guard, and the actual six-test detector. Set

```math
\beta=X^2+B,\qquad T=\operatorname{Tr}x(A),\qquad
t=(\beta^e+T^e)^d+z^2.
```

The parameter $z$ is unrestricted; zero is allowed. The expressions $t$ and $\beta^e+T^e$ add no retained coordinate. With the actual values $V=\operatorname{Tr}(W^{-1})$, $J=\operatorname{Norm}(W^{-1})$ and the original fast physical weight $w$, require

```math
\mathcal R=
\left(1-\frac{V}{1+w}+\frac{J}{(1+w)^2}\right)
\left(1+\frac{1-V/2}{w}\right)\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}.
```

Under weak rational abc with exponent $\kappa$, the mixed-power height inequality gives

```math
h(a)\le C_{\kappa,d}h(a^d+z^2)+O(1),\qquad
C_{\kappa,d}=\frac{(4+\kappa)d-6\kappa}{(2-\kappa)d-4\kappa}.
```

Combining this with the retained two-input and six-contact height bounds gives the strict absorption criterion

```math
q_* = \frac{C_{\kappa,d}}{(e/\kappa-3)(8/\kappa-6)}<2.
```

The proof bounds any erroneous index independently of the fixed shift $B$, and then excludes it for sufficiently large odd $B$. It includes all new denominator and radicand guards at every point-algebra character.

| Height hypothesis | $e$ | $d$ | Exact $q_*$ | Degree of the argument in retained coordinates |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|--:|
| Weak abc at $25/24$; ordinary abc suffices | 8 | 6 | $10000/5187$ | 96 |
| Weak abc at $16/15$ | 10 | 6 | $32/17$ | 120 |
| Weak abc at $121/100$ | 16 | 12 | $40453083/21236816$ | 384 |

Outer amplification can be absorbed into $e$ without increasing the argument degree and with a strictly better absorption coefficient. The [exact certificate](../computation/pole_parameters/single_cover_v56/certificate.json) checks that the displayed degrees are the smallest within this particular even-power height criterion for the three fixed exponents. They are **not full determinant-degree bounds**.

The count is one input, six point coordinates, $z$, and one finite-algebra encoder: **nine**. The point algebra has rank 64, and its one additional quadratic root gives rank 128. This proves a conditional construction criterion, not completeness. For every integer input one must still produce actual points with $A=[X]P$ and a rational $z$ satisfying the square condition. The six contact numerators now have the form $a^2+(c-\theta)b^2$; the inherited theorem with an unrestricted numerator does not supply this restricted prime pattern.

## A weaker realization target with a fixed square-class menu

The same paper permits $\mathcal R$ to lie in any fixed finite collection of positive square classes represented by exceptional-set units. For example, one may allow either a square or twice a square. An odd valuation outside the exceptional set is incompatible with every permitted class, so the pole implication and height arguments remain unchanged.

Each class is encoded using the existing quadratic-branch encoder. A finite product of the resulting polynomials represents the union using the same coordinates, including the same single encoder variable. For two detector blocks take the finite product over pairs of class choices. Thus the **21/9 census** is unchanged. The finite menu and its prime support must be fixed before the input is chosen; no realization theorem for this weaker condition has yet been proved.

The relaxed property keeps the actual detector functions and every required guard. It need not impose the optional local square neighborhoods at all exceptional primes. Imposing all of those neighborhoods would remove the benefit of the nontrivial permitted classes.

A useful equivalent square representative is

```math
\mathcal S=\frac w2\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{W}{(1+w)W-1}\right).
```

It differs from $\mathcal R$ by an explicit nonzero rational square, lies in $(0,1]$ at prepared real characters, and equals one at $W=1$. This identity uses the actual product; freely choosing a different trace does not solve the realization problem.

## A small-coefficient prepared pole block

The [intrinsic pole theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/intrinsic_pole_20261117_v56.tex) proves that

```math
Y^2=P_d(v,s),\qquad
P_d(v,s)=d(d+v^2)^2s^4-d(d-v^2)^2(1-d)^2,
```

alone excludes every negative odd valuation of $d$, at **all primes**, including two. The radicand has degree 9, twelve monomials, content one and exact coefficient height four; its full coefficient table is in the [certificate](../computation/pole_parameters/intrinsic_pole_v56/certificate.json).

Completeness is proved for $0<d<1$ with square reduced denominator, $d\in7\mathbb Q_2^{\times2}$ and $v_2(d)\ge4$, using the stated part of [Burungale, arXiv:2611.16130v1, Theorem B](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16130v1). The proof verifies each squarefree, congruence and reciprocal local-square hypothesis. Independently varying the two elliptic witnesses gives a Zariski-dense projection to $(v,s)$, which permits avoidance of every nonzero guard polynomial over every character of a finite point algebra.

This block has two free coordinates and one finite quadratic root. It does not by itself improve 25 or 13. The manuscript gives sufficient conditions for applying the fixed scaling $d=7b$ to a detector input. A complete source replacement must establish those conditions in its ordered preparation. The direct polynomial implication needs no chart guard, and whole-character avoidance of the radicand only requires $d$ to be a unit; conjugate values $d=1$ are allowed. No full defining-polynomial coefficient or degree improvement is assigned from the isolated block.

## Complete scalar-precision revision

The [precision-transfer theorem](../papers/pole_parameters/precision_transfer_20261117_v56.tex) retains all ten reciprocal-test orders at one and uses

```math
\ell_8(Q)=z(Q)\left(1-\frac25D f(Q)^2\right)
```

in the paired comparisons. Its relative formal error has valuation at least eight in the first formal neighborhood, sufficient for sixth-order comparison tests. The last two scalar tests have order two. The revised Lucas equation uses the squared scalar index.

The two strict global exponents are $7/4$ for a nonzero Lucas residual and $7/6$ for the scalar height. Squared-parameter Lucas growth dominates the latter. The proof includes the complete order of root, neighborhood, prime and auxiliary-index choices, the fixed avoidance support of $1+\rho$, and every new denominator at every character. It defines the integers with **25 total variables** under the retained arithmetic premises, with no additional numerical degree bound.

## Separate negative results

The [order-three coupling obstruction](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/order_three_inert_obstruction_20261117_v56.tex) concerns one deterministic substitution with prescribed root $A=[X]P$. Its thirteen certificate rows cover all 81,421 possible shift residues at an inert prime. A formal-group lift gives infinitely many failing positive integer inputs for every positive odd shift, uniformly over all auxiliary tuples. The scope excludes neither other substitutions nor a free slope.

The [rational-product theorem](../negative_results/papers/pole_parameters/rational_detector_products_20261117_v56.tex) shows that a rational actual conditional detector product satisfying the pole implication has no nonexceptional odd contact. Faltings's theorem then restricts the argument to a finite set. Making both products rational consequently permits only finitely many integer inputs. It leaves actual nonrational products and individually chosen rational witnesses open.
