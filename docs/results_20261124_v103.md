## Polynomial division shares the contact denominators

The existing contact progression supplies square and fourth-power values
at thirteen or eleven first contacts and at one additional endpoint.
Their inverse-weight equations used a private combination register at
each contact. Polynomial division removes the highest numerator term,
after an affine change of the inverse-weight coordinate. The remaining
denominator has degree eight in the contact index, so its nine common
coefficients replace fourteen or twelve private registers.

The [proof](../undecidability/quadratic_reduction_v103/contact_denominator_compression.tex)
([PDF](../undecidability/quadratic_reduction_v103/contact_denominator_compression.pdf))
gives exact inverse coordinate maps. They divide only by an already
guarded nonzero gauge and a fixed nonzero rational constant. In
particular the argument covers zero inverse weights and zero spacing.
It is a bijection of complete rational zero sets, with no new arithmetic
selection argument or assumption. All thirteen / eleven integer
certificates remain. Squaring and summing the quadratic residuals gives
degree exactly four.

The [complete coefficient tables and checker](../undecidability/quadratic_reduction_v103)
record every changed and unchanged row, coefficient multiplier, coordinate
occurrence, guard and hash. The independent checker imports no compiler.
It verifies 42 / 36 changed old rows, 767 / 946 unchanged rows, and nine
new coefficient definitions. A separate Vandermonde argument shows why
nine coefficients cannot be improved within the unchanged progression
with rational constant affine evaluation; it is not a general lower bound.

## A new local integrality criterion and a different source

The [source checkpoint](../undecidability/source_frontier_v103/README.md)
proves that, for integral a, T and d with |a| at least two, the equations

```math
d=(a-2)c+T,\qquad d^2-(a^2-1)c^2=1
```

force rational c to be integral. Their denominator constraints first
give a denominator at most three. An elementary Pell descent excludes
denominators two and three; the threshold is sharp at denominator four.
Thus the current c certificate can equivalently be aimed at d, but the
remaining source has not yet been shown to force either one independently.
No integer certificate is removed by this lemma.

The checkpoint also verifies a primary classical source of Jones and
Matiyasevich using two natural unknowns in an inequality with iterated
fixed-base exponentiation, and three in an equation. Only one unknown
occurs in exponents. This offers a different research direction, but
no polynomial replacement of all its exponentials or quartic count has
been established. The exact binary-digit identity behind the present
power-of-two condition is proved separately; it does not settle that
condition's independence from the full source.
