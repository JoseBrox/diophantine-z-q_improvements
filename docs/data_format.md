# Polynomial data formats

## The 456-variable quartic

`computation/quartic/polynomial-456/residuals.json.gz` is a losslessly compressed JSON description of the 426 quadratic equations. Its coordinate list fixes every coordinate's index and mathematical name; index zero is the input. A term records its integer coefficient, the coordinate indices in its monomial, and the exponent `sPower` of the fixed constant $S=3^{52832906}$.

`polynomial.json.gz` records every collected coefficient of the sum of their squares. Its 27,791 terms over $\mathbb Z[S]$ give 27,784 coordinate monomials after equal coordinate monomials are combined. The coefficient bound in the paper proves that none of these coefficients vanishes after the specified value of $S$ is substituted. `sPower` is a compact encoding of an integer coefficient, not an additional quantified coordinate.

`transformation-certificate.json` records the coordinate substitutions from the baseline system. The separate checker reconstructs them in named coordinates and expands the full sum of squares. The producer and checker do not share polynomial-manipulation code.

## The 29- and 31-variable constructions

Each `polynomial-specification.json` supplies a finite exact expression for an integer polynomial. The specification includes the base coordinates, triangular monic algebra relations, polynomial coefficient expressions, guard factors, separating-form range, degree alternatives, determinant formulas, and finite products. The final reciprocal coordinate is included in the total variable count.

An indexed product with a very large finite endpoint remains part of the polynomial's definition. Its constant coefficients are generated by the specified exact elliptic addition algorithm. These constants and all determinant entries are specified by finite arithmetic; they are not quantified variables. The complete expanded coefficient list would be enormous and is not claimed to have been materialized.

The bound fields record certified upper bounds for total degree and coefficient height. A coefficient-expression node count is a representation size, not an expanded monomial count. All denominators, nonzero guards, zero-root alternatives, and degree drops are included in the finite specification.

The 29-variable specification SHA-256 is `aac8f38f50c6780bec58bcc7057b43a324270e4f7ad6d439151773f512227072`. Its mathematical-data hash after omitting only the `bounds` field is `24cacbb45176225d8e8278edeaf170cada85b0397134c69867ad8641a26f2823`.

## Proof-location metadata

Frozen source graphs retain identifiers for the lemmas used in generating them. These strings are mathematical provenance metadata; no checker executes or opens those paths. The complete current proofs are in the [small-variable paper](../papers/few_variables/few_variables_20261107_v7.pdf), particularly the finite-algebra theorem, the input-map lemma, the elementary Lucas lemma, and the coefficient-bound section. The computational manifest maps the package to that paper.

## Exact finite constants

The height-constant data contain the complete list of primes at most 76,176 and the unsigned big-endian binary encoding of $H_{19}$. The checker independently generates both objects using a sieve and trial division. Integer powers and rational arithmetic are exact throughout; floating-point approximations do not certify any decisive inequality.
