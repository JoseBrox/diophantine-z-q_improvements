## The zero branch no longer requires the sign square

The retained strengthened margin is

```math
k^2-4(c-kY)^2-C_*^2>0.
```

If s=0, the small Pell norm and integrality force `k=+1` or `-1`;
the margin then forces `c=C_*=0`. Consequently `A0-1` divides `5^60`.
But

```math
A_0-1=(b-1)H_0(b),\qquad
H_0(b)=\frac{(1+\cdots+b^8)+8(1+\cdots+b^4)+1}{2}.
```

On the existing tail `b>x>32z`, divisibility would give `b-1=5^a`
with `a>=3`. Then `H0(b)=25 (mod125)`, so it has 5-adic valuation
exactly two, while `H0(b)>25`. It cannot be a divisor of `5^60`.
Thus s is nonzero before any reciprocal or first-Pell index recovery.

## An alternating truncation replaces the positive-sign argument

The retained congruences and size bounds permit either first-Pell
sign. They force

```math
|bw|=2^{2r+1},\qquad
\operatorname{sgn}(c)=\operatorname{sgn}(w),\qquad
\operatorname{sgn}(s)=\operatorname{sgn}(w)^{r+1}.
```

Set `sigma=sign(w)`, `U=abs(w)M`, and `Z=abs(s)M`. The normalized
Pell estimate holds for both signs and approximates

```math
\rho=\frac{(U+\sigma)^{2r}}{U^r}.
```

The new signed-prefix bounds give `M>=4b`. This makes the binomial
tail beyond degree r smaller than one quarter; the normalized Pell
error is also smaller than one quarter. The existing strict
half-integer approximation therefore forces the exact integer identity

```math
Z=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{2r}{j}\sigma^j U^{r-j}.
```

Reducing modulo M yields `M | binomial(2r,r)` for either sign. The
negative-w case requires this alternating sum; one cannot simply
reuse the positive formula with the same signed witnesses.

## Recovering the fixed power and rebuilding a positive source

All auxiliary-power estimates now use absolute values. They give
`abs(A)>abs(s)`, `abs(A-1)>L+6r`, and `abs(m_*)>abs(s)`, where
`L=5^60`. The auxiliary Pell index is at most `6r`; its congruence
modulo `abs(A-1)` forces the positive coordinate branch and index L.
The final congruence modulo `abs(m_*)` then gives `q=b^L`, including
when A was initially negative. The zero index and both trace signs
are covered explicitly.

The resulting positive power of two b and central-binomial divisibility
allow the original binomial theorem to reconstruct fresh positive
witnesses for the same `(r,M,b)`. The one-quotient Pell and short-power
completeness theorems supply the remaining positive integers and the
stronger margin. The old sign square is then restored on this newly
constructed positive source.

This proves **equivalence of the coding-prefix and instance projections**.
It does not claim that every signed auxiliary solution extends to the
old sign-selected zero set. Exact negative-w examples in the checker
show why that distinction matters.
