# Independent review of the actual-trace genus argument

The proof applies to the actual nine-shift derivative detector, with the
elliptic multiplication relation imposed and every original point and
denominator guard retained. It does not prove failure of the rational
realization criterion.

For `a>10`, the nine positive numbers `a+c` are distinct and nonzero;
their negatives supply eighteen different contacts. At the contact with
sign `epsilon`, exactly one factor of `F` vanishes, and
`F_z/F_a=-epsilon`. The remaining factors are nonzero, so
`u=-18F/F_a` has a simple zero with derivative `18*epsilon`.

For each fixed guarded auxiliary point, the first four reciprocal tests
are finite and nonzero in both geometric slots. Each corresponding
reciprocal detector factor has a simple zero at `u=0`. Consequently
`V=O(u^4)`, even if the leading terms in the trace cancel. Substitution
gives `C=2u+O(u^2)` and contact derivative `36*epsilon`. This includes
zero values of the last two comparison tests.

These simple zeros make `C` a nonsquare even over an algebraic closure.
The normalized double cover is therefore geometrically integral, with
at least eighteen branch points. Riemann--Hurwitz gives genus at least
eight. The rational points over those known contacts are outside the
required open set because their weight is zero.

For the varying-point statement, a rational curve projects constantly
to the proper abelian surface `Res(E)`: a morphism from the projective
line to an abelian variety is constant in characteristic zero. A curve
with varying slope would then map to the high-genus normalized fiber,
again contradicting Riemann--Hurwitz. A constant slope gives a finite
fiber in the quadratic ordinate. Finite point-chart covers cannot add
a curve over a point. The argument concerns rational curves through
the guarded locus and does not imply finiteness as the cyclic auxiliary
point varies.

The exact checker was rerun after publication typography was fixed.
The contact identities and first-order expansions agree with this proof.
The geometric assertions follow from the proof, not from finite samples.
