# Independent review of the number-field transfer lemmas

The two manuscripts were checked against their complete proofs.

## Adjacent contacts after descent

Multiplication by t kills both torsion translates and carries P to Q in E(K). At a good base prime, reduction commutes with multiplication and residue-field extension is injective on reduced points. Consequently the two contacts imply hQ=0 and (h-c)Q=0 in the same reduced group, hence cQ=0. This argument never divides by t; residue characteristics dividing t cause no exception. It also needs neither splitting nor distinct places.

For fixed nonzero c, the point cQ is nonidentity. Outside the finite discriminant support of a fixed integral model, identity reduction is equivalent to negative abscissa valuation, so only its finite denominator support is possible. Equal positive Mordell–Weil ranks imply that E(L)/E(K) is finite. The quoted rank-stability theorem is Zywina, arXiv:2505.16960v1, Theorem 1.2; the author-hosted primary manuscript was read directly.

The conclusion excludes the specified adjacent-contact substitution. It does not exclude every integrality detector or the desired number-field definition.

## Finite corrections and an integral basis

Northcott finiteness makes A=S minus O_K finite under the stated height assumption. The finite omitted integer set B is disjoint from A. For a nonsquare delta in K, the equation

`b(X) * (P(X,Y)^2 - delta*(V*a(X)-1)^2) = 0`

defines exactly (S minus A) union B. Both directions hold also when either finite set is empty; the nonzero factor b(X) forces the two residuals to vanish separately. The degree is at most |B|+2*max(D,|A|+1), using N+1 total coordinates.

The corrected set lies between Z and O_K. An integral basis then gives O_K as the sum of its d scalar multiples. Eliminating the last basis coefficient linearly leaves exactly d(N+1) coordinates including the input. A field norm from a degree-d extension combines d residuals without new coordinates; its vanishing forces every residual to vanish and multiplies the degree bound by d. For d=1 the norm is the identity. A degree-d extension exists by an Eisenstein construction at a finite prime.

These are existence results. They supply neither the required initial Diophantine set nor effective discovery of the finite exceptions. No number-field counterexample is claimed.
