## One progression for the general contact families

After the root points and aggregate shifts are fixed, each integer block
has a finite rational contact list. If their union lies in an interval of
length W, choose an integer D>W. Translating the i-th list by -iD makes the
lists pairwise disjoint. This choice occurs before the finite-family prime
supplier is applied, so its finite avoidance and common-index congruences
remain available. The spacing is quantified; it is not a fixed numerical
constant asserted to separate every possible tuple.

The [complete proof](../undecidability/general_contact_v94/affine_contact_progressions.tex)
([PDF](../undecidability/general_contact_v94/affine_contact_progressions.pdf))
uses the common contact arguments `lambda_i=lambda+iD`. Their stored first
arguments, centered squares and centered fourth powers become affine in
the old first-block registers and seven new common registers. For twelve
blocks, these seven registers replace thirty-three private registers,
saving **26 variables and 16 rows**.

For the general chart, the stored coordinates satisfy

```math
t_i=\lambda_i/\rho,\qquad
u_i=(\lambda_i-1)^2,\qquad f_i=(\lambda_i-1)^4.
```

Writing `epsilon=1/rho`, introduce `d,v,w,a_1,...,a_4` and use

```math
\epsilon^2v=d(t-\epsilon),\quad \epsilon^2w=d^2,
\quad a_1=4uv,\quad a_2=2uw+4v^2,\quad
a_3=4vw,\quad a_4=w^2.
```

The substitutions are

```math
t_i=t+id,\quad u_i=u+2iv+i^2w,\quad
f_i=f+ia_1+i^2a_2+i^3a_3+i^4a_4.
```

The specified chart has different scaling and center; its six equations
are given separately in the proof. In both charts every omitted square
or fourth-power equation has an exact polynomial reconstruction identity.
No new nonzero guard is needed for the spacing, and all substituted rows
remain quadratic. Completeness follows with the retained supplier and
fixed-place preparation: the finitely many offsets are fixed before the
common contact parameter tends to infinity in the required local senses.

## The binomial block forces w and s to be positive

The [signed-source theorem](../undecidability/source_repair_v93/signed_binomial_positivity.tex)
([PDF](../undecidability/source_repair_v93/signed_binomial_positivity.pdf))
proves that the corrected binomial block with `r>=1`, `M>=16`,
`2<=b<=2M`, and `s!=0` forces `w>0`, `s>0`, `c>0`, and
`bw=2^(2r+1)`. It treats both signs of the two Pell parameters. The
negative-c branch gives an inverse power-of-two congruence; an explicit
uniform size bound excludes that branch. The remaining congruence forces
the exact power of two, and then both positive signs.

This removes the two three-coordinate positivity wrappers for w and s.
Adding `s*s_inverse=1` costs one coordinate and one quadratic row, for a
net saving of **five variables and one row**. The nonzero guard is
essential: the manuscript gives an exact zero-s counterexample. The
reciprocal predicate is reconstructed without circularity: the strict
closeness inequality first forces k and c nonzero, and only then does
the existing reciprocal equation recover the integral Pell witness.

For the universal source, `M=n^2` and `n=b^(16*5^60)`, with `b>=2`, so
the size hypotheses hold. This theorem applies to the corrected n^6
block, and does not silently repair the historical n^2 block.

inside the integer certificates and private positivity coordinates in
the outer source. Their reconstruction arguments compose, giving
**1043 / 1223**. The abc fixed-offset construction and the same signed

metadata repair; the already corrected polynomial rows do not change.
