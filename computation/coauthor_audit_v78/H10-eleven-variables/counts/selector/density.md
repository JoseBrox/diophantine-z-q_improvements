# Why simply deleting the final rationality witness fails

Fix an admissible coefficient shift in the ordinary-abc 11-coordinate construction, or either weak-abc 13-coordinate construction of Brox at d3a8c22. Let $b$ denote the number of remaining coordinates after deletion of the final determinant variable. These are
$$
\begin{array}{cl}
b=10&X,\quad \text{six point-chart coordinates},\quad z,\quad v,s,\\
b=12&X,\quad \text{six point-chart coordinates},\quad \lambda,\quad
\text{two independent pairs of rational pole parameters}.
\end{array}
$$
Under the arithmetic premises used to supply the source witnesses, the complete set of free-coordinate tuples supplied by the integer completeness procedure is Zariski dense in $\mathbb A^b_{\mathbb C}$. Here the set includes all permitted choices of the primes, reference progression and pole witnesses. It does not mean one arbitrarily selected tuple per integer.

Consequently any polynomial in the remaining $b$ coordinates that vanishes on every such tuple is the zero polynomial. In particular no root-of-unity filter, resultant, bounded primitive element, fixed specialization of the selector, substitution of an existing rational coordinate or function for the selector, or finite disjunction of such polynomials can produce a defining hypersurface while retaining every old prepared base tuple. For a rational substitution, clear its denominator on its domain. A finite union of proper hypersurfaces is still proper. This consequence is a statement about the two specified free-coordinate interfaces. It gives no general lower bound of 11 or 13 on possible Diophantine presentations.

## Compact multiplication graphs

Put $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and $A=\mathrm{Res}_{K/\mathbb Q}E$. The conjugates of the source generator $P$ on the split complex square of the elliptic curve are linearly independent modulo torsion over its CM ring. This is the independence lemma of the pinned weighted trace-height paper, applied to $P=2R_0$. Hence the cyclic subgroup generated by $P$ is Zariski dense in $A_{\mathbb C}$. Indeed, the connected component of a proper algebraic subgroup of a square of this CM elliptic curve is killed by a nonzero row of two endomorphisms up to isogeny, contradicting independence. The Zariski closure of any nonzero integral multiple of the cyclic group is likewise the whole surface, since a nonzero multiplication map is finite surjective.

Let $T$ be the identity component of the compact topological closure of $\mathbb ZP$ in $A(\mathbb R)$. Multiplying $P$ by the finite component-group order $d$ gives a point generating a dense subgroup of the connected compact torus $T$. In particular $T$ is Zariski dense in $A_{\mathbb C}$, even if its real dimension is less than the real dimension of the surface. An open subset of any coset of $T$ in the orbit closure is also Zariski dense: a polynomial restriction is real analytic on the connected coset, and analytic continuation extends its vanishing from an open set. One may restrict to a chart, or clear its rational-function denominators first.

For fixed $n$, write $\mathcal G_n=\{(H,[n]H):H\in A_{\mathbb C}\}$. The Zariski closure in $\mathbb A^1\times A_{\mathbb C}^3$ of
$$
\bigcup_{n>0}\{(n,[n]P)\}\times \mathcal G_n
$$
is the entire product. An elementary argument also explains why the input $n$ can appear in the candidate polynomial. Choose any target $R,S,V\in T$ on an open chart. There are positive integers $n_j\to\infty$ divisible by $d$ with $[n_j]P\to R$. For each $j$ there exists $S_j\in T$ with $S_j\to S$ and $[n_j]S_j=V$. Indeed, the lifts of the preimages of $V$ form a translate of a mesh of spacing $1/n_j$ on a real torus. In local abscissa coordinates a putative polynomial relation has the form $\sum_{i=0}^m n^i f_i(R,S,V)=0$ with regular or rational $f_i$. Evaluate at $n_j,[n_j]P,S_j,[n_j]S_j$, divide by $n_j^m$, and take the limit away from denominator poles. The leading coefficient $f_m$ vanishes on an open dense part of $T^3$, hence identically. Descending on $m$ proves the assertion on the charts in use. The same proof works for the restricted progression of positive multiples of $d$.

## Applying the source's choice order

Fix a positive integer $n$ and the actual root point $R=[n]P$. All height scales and shifted prime forms are now fixed. The supplier allows arbitrarily large prescribed real slopes at this fixed input, hence infinitely many distinct slopes. Once a particular prepared slope is fixed, the permissible reference integers $h$ lie in congruence classes with a real neighborhood condition. Choose one permissible class and a nonempty real open neighborhood of a successful return. Refining a progression by finite congruences still leaves a coset whose connected orbit closure is $T$, since nonzero multiplication on $T$ is onto. Within the real neighborhood its returns contain a relatively dense orbit subset. Thus the corresponding $(H,U)=([h]P,[n]H)$ are Zariski dense in $\mathcal G_n$. Shrinking the allowed neighborhoods to exclude finitely many chart poles does not affect the argument. The original proof's prescribed bounds and guards are open at the selected successful tuples.

For each such tuple, the actual rational witness pairs for an intrinsic pole are Zariski dense in the $\mathbb A^2$ of pole parameters by the source theorem. In the two-pole route the two independently prepared pairs have a Zariski-dense product in $\mathbb A^4$. The source permits deleting finitely many proper guard loci after these choices. A polynomial vanishing on all old tuples therefore vanishes identically in its pole variables, then identically along the reference graph at each fixed permitted slope, then identically in its slope variable for each fixed $n$. The compact multiplication argument applies to every remaining coefficient.

Finally, each pair of unrestricted point coordinates in these sources is either the two coordinates of an abscissa in $K$, or those of a square root of this abscissa in $K$. On complexification, passing from square-root coordinates to abscissa coordinates is the independent squaring of the two embeddings. If a nonzero polynomial in the upper coordinates vanished on all the chosen square-root lifts, take its product under the sign changes in each squared coordinate. This product is nonzero, is a polynomial in the downstairs abscissas, and vanishes on all their projections. That contradicts the preceding proof. Pullback from abscissa coordinates to $A_{\mathbb C}^3$ is injective on polynomials, since abscissa is a dominant finite map on each elliptic factor.

## Remaining route for a numerical reduction

A 10- or 12-coordinate proposal using the present base must choose new, correlated preparations on a proper algebraic subset, with at least one rational point in it over each integer input. Density by itself neither supplies nor excludes such points. In particular it does not exclude a special curve on an individual intrinsic pole surface or a new relation tying the pole realization to the prime slope. What it excludes is deducing completeness of such a slice from the unchanged open-set and congruence avoidance statements. Those statements supply rational points outside prescribed closed subsets; landing on one is a new arithmetic obligation.
