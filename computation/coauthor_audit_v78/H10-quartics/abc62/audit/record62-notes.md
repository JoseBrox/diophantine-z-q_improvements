# The 62-coordinate abc version

The checker `verify_abc.py` takes the upstream v65 `quadratic-family.json` and either new record directory. It checks 50 equations in the 62-coordinate version and independently reconstructs the complete quartic from them. The unchanged local and real realization conditions and the separate zero-input witness are given in `README.md`.

Here \(C=s-S\), \(I=t-T_H\) and \(J,K\) are the source reciprocal products. After undoing the coordinate map the field polynomial is
\[
 W=1+c C^4+c^2J^2-c^3\bar I^4-c^4\bar K^2.
\]
The two stored pairs of field components are \(C^2\pm c\bar I^2\) and \(J\pm c\bar K\). Each pair recovers both physical elements by division by two and by the guarded nonzero \(2c\). These invertible changes and the remaining equations remove the separate field-valued quadratic helper of the 64-coordinate presentation.

Take a consumed good odd prime of depth \(\nu\). The scalar gate, two pole equations and Laurent chart are unchanged and give \(v_p(c)=-\nu\), \(v_p(D)>0\), and an embedding with \(v(W)\ge0\). This last conclusion follows even when \(W\) itself is zero at an embedding: otherwise poles at both embeddings would force \(v_p(\mathrm{Nm}(W-1))<v_p(\mathrm{Tr}(W))\). A globally vanishing trace is excluded by the literal shifted-norm equation. At the successful embedding, the four nonconstant terms, when nonzero, have valuations in the four different classes \(-\nu,-2\nu,-3\nu,-4\nu\pmod4\). This uses even valuation of \(J,K\). A negative least valuation would be unique, contradicting \(v(W)\ge0\). Every nonconstant term therefore has nonnegative valuation. For its underlying test \(T\) this gives \(m v(T)\ge e\nu>0\), so all four required tests have positive valuation. A tie with the constant at valuation zero has no effect on this implication. The v65 arithmetic conclusion follows with its own cutoff under the ordinary-abc and inherited source premises.

At a selected prime for a prepared nonzero integer, the successful embedding has \(v(C),v(\bar I)\ge3\) and \(v(J),v(\bar K)\ge4\), giving \(W\equiv1\). At the opposite embedding the points reduce to \(\pm R_0,\pm[X]R_0\) as in v65, and the selected prime avoids the abscissa numerator/denominator primes and the divisors of \(2X\). Both reciprocal products are units there. The last term then has valuation \(-4\), so a negative term exists; the unique-negative-minimum argument forces \(v(W)<0\). Accordingly the trace cancels this pole from \(\mathrm{Nm}(W-1)\) and yields \(v_p(D)>0\). With \(X=0\) the source zero branch instead gives \(W=1\) in the field and \(D=0\) directly. Replace the 64-specific factor argument by these observations throughout `README.md`; both actual inputs still satisfy every condition of the large-pole realization lemma.
