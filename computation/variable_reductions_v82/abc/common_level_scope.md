# Scope of a common-level reduction for the two general pole blocks

This is an applicability note, not a new variable bound. It develops the source notes of Gabe Conant with Astra 6.2.

## The retained theorem is a one-class theorem

The unmodified source `sources/hexagon/jaswal_row5a_audit_and_simultaneous_twists_20261113.tex`, theorem `thm:sim`, fixes one squarefree β≡1 mod 8 and a pairwise-coprime family of admissible complements. Its common level gives rank-one curves and a prescribed Kummer class with first coordinate β on every curve. The explicit scope remark excludes numerical-coordinate sharing. The published Burungale theorem likewise fixes one β, now in class 7 modulo8, for its two twists. Neither assertion permits distinct input classes β₀,β₁ on one rank-one base curve.

The common-class three-parameter transfer is already proved in `papers/pole_parameters/common_twists_20261114_v30.tex`. It does not apply to the distinct square classes of the actual two detectors. The common-normalized-conic-parameter problem and its guard accounting are already isolated in `papers/pole_parameters/common_parameter_guard_reduction_20261116_v42.tex`; that paper correctly keeps simultaneous rational realization open.

## Rank-one obstruction to the naive distinct-class strengthening

Let n>0 be squarefree, n≡5 mod 8, and suppose E_n:y²=x³−n²x has rank one. The abscissa square-class homomorphism has torsion image generated by −1 and n. If R is a generator of the free group modulo torsion and α=[x(R)], every nonzero abscissa class belongs to

    {1,−1,n,−n,α,−α,nα,−nα}.

Among positive nontrivial classes with a specified 2-adic square class ε∈{1,7}, at most one class occurs. Indeed the positive classes among the torsion part are 1 and n, of 2-adic classes 1 and 5. After choosing the positive sign of α, its two positive translates are α and nα, whose 2-adic square classes differ by 5. If ε=1, the torsion class 1 is excluded by the nontrivial-input assumption; if ε=7, neither torsion class qualifies. Thus two distinct positive nonsquare input classes, both prepared in class 1 or both in class 7 at 2, cannot both occur on one rank-one level n≡5 mod 8.

This directly prevents extending the retained theorem by merely asking the same rank-one base curve to serve both distinct input classes. A different common-parameter construction is not ruled out: sharing the normalized rational conic parameter gives different conic values and need not share the elliptic level.

## A quadric alternative and its soundness limitation

For a fixed nonsquare a>0, the norm-one, trace-(2+b) equation in the quaternion algebra (a,b) is

    a x²+b y²−ab z²=b(b+4).

At an odd unramified prime p where(a/p)=−1 and v_p(b)<0 is odd, the quaternion algebra is division and the trace 2+b is nonintegral, so the equation has no Q_p point. If b has square denominator, every such ramified prime has positive odd v_p(b); the discriminant b(b+4) is nonsquare and the trace condition is locally admissible. Suitable preparation at the finitely many ramified/fixed places and Hasse–Minkowski therefore yield a two-free-coordinate pole predicate on those inert primes.

Sharing x between two such blocks would give an intersection of two quadrics in P⁵ with three free coordinates, rather than two genus-one pole surfaces. However, with a=7 this implication only covers primes inert in Q(√7). The general source's soundness radical includes arbitrary odd contact primes, not only the good primes selected in its completeness construction. No valid inert-prime radical-height replacement has been established. This already prevents substituting the quadric for the native pole; no common-x completeness theorem is asserted either.

The precise remaining 24-coordinate target is still an actual-source simultaneous realization theorem for a three-parameter conjunction of the two native pole inputs. Zariski density of the two separate witness sets does not impose their parameter equality.
