# Review of the ten-coordinate rational-subfield construction

## Claim and scope

The finite-algebra compression retains the amplified descent assumptions:
a fixed number field K, an elliptic curve E/Q with positive rank unchanged
over K, the stated six-point truncated-height inequality (supplied by abc
over K), and Burungale's published prepared class-seven pole theorem.
The conclusion is a definition of **Q inside K in ten total K-coordinates**,
with integer coefficients and an ineffective finite correction. It is
not a ten-variable definition of Z over Q.

The original descent architecture is due to Pasten and Vidaux. The
amplification and compressed encoding are subsequent project extensions.
The rational-integer construction used only for composition retains the
attribution to notes of **Gabe Conant with Astra 6.2** and all its original
arithmetic assumptions.

## Rational preimages and the ordered completeness argument

Soundness uses mE(K) contained in E(Q). Completeness may use the smaller
positive-rank subgroup mE(Q). The all-rational real and finite-place
approximation proof works unchanged in this subgroup: its intersection
with any finite collection of formal neighborhoods has finite index and
contains a point of infinite order. All four raw preimages can therefore
be chosen in E(Q).

Use an integral short Weierstrass equation. With its four rational
abscissas fixed, the degree-sixteen point algebra has four independent
ordinate signs. Since multiplication by m commutes with negation and
xi=-x/y is odd, these characters only replace the two approximation
ratios lambda and beta by their signs. All point denominator and image
coordinate guards remain nonzero because they hold on both signs of
each actual point. The standard multiplication-chart denominators vanish
only over the identity; no additional raw point fibre is discarded.

Choose lambda avoiding 0, plus or minus 1, and plus or minus g, then
choose beta avoiding plus or minus t. This is finite avoidance in the
already proved approximation lemma. For rational t the chosen cubic
map has no zero, pole, or value minus one. Hence each of the two pole
inputs is nonzero at **every** geometric point-algebra character. The
first ratio is fixed before preparing the second; every numerator and
denominator prime of the actual first ratio is included. There is no
assumption of a common denominator point or simultaneous unproved
square realization.

## Actual class-seven witnesses are dense

Each actual prepared pole input b admits a multiplier mu from the stated
menu for which d=b/mu is positive, below one, has square reduced
denominator, lies in 7 times the nonzero two-adic squares, and has
two-adic valuation at least four. These are exactly the assumptions
of the density theorem in `intrinsic_pole_20261117_v56.tex`.

That theorem uses the published Burungale Theorem B, with beta seven
and eta one modulo eight. It fixes the two resulting positive-rank
elliptic curves and varies points in their prescribed abscissa
square-class cosets independently. The first curve gives infinitely
many values of v; for each of them the second gives infinitely many
values of s. Thus the actual rational witness pairs are Zariski dense,
even with respect to polynomials over the algebraic closure. No
square-class-one strengthening is used.

For every other point-algebra character the input d' is nonzero, and

```math
P_{d'}(v,s)=d'(d'+v^2)^2s^4-d'(d'-v^2)^2(1-d')^2
```

is a nonzero polynomial in v,s: its leading coefficient in s is
nonzero as a polynomial in v. The product over finitely many characters
is nonzero. Actual rational witnesses can therefore avoid all these
radicand zeros, simultaneously with any other nonzero polynomial
guards. Applying this argument independently to each pole block gives
two nonzero terminal radicands at every point character. This is
avoidance on an existing dense witness family, not an assertion that
density imposes a prescribed equality.

## Monic algebra and cleared divisions

The base coordinates are the input, four elliptic abscissas, and the
four pole parameters. The point algebra is generated by four monic
quadratic ordinate equations. The reduced odd map xi composed with [m]
is y*u_m(x)/v_m(x). Rationalizing a quotient of two such expressions
gives lambda=Lambda/L, with Lambda=Y1*Y2*u1*v2 and scalar
L=F2*u2*v1. Similarly beta=B/M has scalar denominator M. The square
S=Lambda^2 is scalar. The first source input therefore has scalar
denominator L^2*Q*P^3, and the second M^2*L^3*S^2, using
1/Lambda^3=Lambda/S^2. The checker reconstructs both identities
directly. All denominators are guarded by the displayed scalar
point-chart and contact factors; no algebra-valued inverse is hidden.

If a normalized pole input is A/C, choose the terminal radicand as
C^6 P_{A/C}(v,s). The polynomial P has input degree at most five,
so this is polynomial in the point algebra. At every character with
C nonzero, the terminal root divided by C^3 is precisely the original
pole root. Conversely any original rational pole root gives the
cleared root by multiplication by C^3. This even clearing is essential:
arbitrary clearing of a square equation could change its square class.

Adjoining these two monic quadratic roots gives a free rank-64 algebra.
The guarded point radicands and norms of terminal radicands make every
accepted specialization etale. At the actual rational witnesses this
algebra has a rational character. No extra variable is used for its
six algebraic generators; they will be recovered from a primitive
element.

## A finite separator menu suffices uniformly

For the six generators r1,...,r6, take

```math
\alpha_j=\sum_{i=1}^{6}j^{i-1}r_i,
\qquad 1\le j\le 10081.
```

Distinct geometric characters differ on at least one generator. Their
values on alpha_j therefore differ by a nonzero polynomial in j of
degree at most five. There are 2016 character pairs. Consequently at
most 10080 integers can be forbidden, and the displayed menu contains
a primitive element at every etale specialization. This is a universal
argument over every characteristic-zero field, not a finite random
search or a claim that one fixed j works on every fibre.

Let Delta_j be the determinant of the power-basis columns
1,alpha_j,...,alpha_j^63 in the stated free basis. Its nonvanishing
expresses primitivity exactly. Let G_* be the product of all scalar
guards, including the finite-exception correction, and set
G_j=G_* Delta_j. Then use the single encoder coordinate z in

```math
F_j(z)=\det\bigl(G_j(zI-M_{\alpha_j})-I\bigr).
```

If G_j=0, this determinant is det(-I)=1 because the rank is 64.
Thus it has no false zero on a missing guard. If G_j is nonzero and
F_j(z)=0 over K, then z-1/G_j is a K-valued eigenvalue of alpha_j.
Primitivity identifies the specialized algebra with K[T]/(chi(T));
a K-root of chi gives a K-algebra character and recovers every
ordinate and terminal root in K. In the other direction, an actual
character chi gives z=chi(alpha_j)+1/G_j. A zero primitive eigenvalue
is allowed; no inversion of alpha_j occurs. The independent checker
includes such a fixture to detect accidental replacement by a
reciprocal encoder.

Take the product of these polynomials over the finite separator menu
and over the sixteen choices for each of the two pole multipliers.
Over a field, vanishing of the product selects an actual branch.
Every branch enforces its own guards. The chosen maps have integer
polynomial entries, so the result is already an integer polynomial.
For every rational prime, a factor is one if its guard vanishes
identically modulo that prime; otherwise its leading encoder coefficient
is the nonzero polynomial G_j^64. Thus every factor is primitive, and
Gauss's lemma makes the product primitive. Its finite
determinant specification is complete; a fully expanded polynomial
and a uniform numerical coefficient height are not claimed.

## Count, correction, composition and limits

The count is one input, four abscissas, four pole parameters and one
encoder: **10**. The same finite correction as in the amplified descent
excludes irrational exceptions and has no rational root. It is part
of the existing guard, so costs no coordinate. Its coefficients and
the chosen multiplication index may be ineffective. The alternative
power-shift correction likewise changes degree, not the count.

An N-coordinate definition of Z over Q yields a 10N-coordinate
definition of Z inside K by restricting **every** original coordinate,
including the input. An integral-basis construction gives 10[K:Q]N
coordinates for the ring of integers. For the project's N=11 source
this is 110 and 110[K:Q], retaining all of that source's arithmetic
assumptions. In the quadratic case the latter is 220.

The source rank hypothesis is satisfied by the cited quadratic
rank-stability theorem. No theorem for all higher-degree extensions
is inferred from rank-one existence over a number field. The finite
algebra calculation proves neither the arithmetic premises nor an
effective height bound. It does not resolve the separate 25/11
actual-input realization problem or the general 165/180 quartic
compilation problem.
