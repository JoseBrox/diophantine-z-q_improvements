# Independent review of amplified rationality descent

This review concerns a definition of the rational subfield inside a fixed
number field. It does not prove a reduction below either 25 or 11 variables
for defining the rational integers over the rationals. The independent
reviewer developed the approximation and conic reductions separately from
the manuscript author, then checked the resulting common construction.

The reviewed manuscript is
papers/number_fields/amplified_rationality_descent_20261119_v81.tex.
The exact reviewed source and checker hashes are recorded separately in
manual_review.json after the manuscript is final. The source-hash receipt
is not regenerated by the finite checker.

## Assumptions and primary-source scope

The construction assumes an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb Q$ with
$0<\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{rank}E(K)$, together with
the specified truncated-counting lower bound over $K$. The usual abc
conjecture over $K$ supplies the required lower bound for the reduced
six-point divisor used below. The local pole predicate uses the published
simultaneous-twist input of Burungale's Theorem B; the finite checker does
not establish that arithmetic theorem.

Pasten and Vidaux, [arXiv:2611.24748v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24748v1),
Theorem 1.1 and Sections 3--5, give the stable-rank rationality argument
that motivates this construction. Their passage through an
integer-containing set is replaced here by direct completeness for every
rational input. The amplified second test and the use of zeros and poles
are further changes whose proofs must be supplied independently.

Burungale, [arXiv:2611.16130v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16130v1),
Definition 3.1 and Proposition 3.14, provide the original predicate
$\Phi$, including $\Phi(0)$ and completeness for rational inputs with
square reduced denominator. The present sixteen-coordinate formula uses
the narrower prepared-input consequence of Theorem B proved in the
project's intrinsic-pole paper, rather than asserting that its new
predicate has the original full completeness property.

Zywina, [arXiv:2505.16960v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16960v1),
Theorem 1.2, states that every quadratic extension $L/F$ of number fields
admits elliptic curves over $F$ of rank one over both fields. Taking
$F=\mathbb Q$ gives the required rank-stable curve when $K/\mathbb Q$ is
quadratic. For a general number field $K$, the stable curve defined over
$\mathbb Q$ remains a separate hypothesis. Theorem 1.1, which gives a
rank-one curve over each number field, does not remove that hypothesis.

## Rational approximation, including the real place

Since the ranks agree, the finitely generated quotient
$E(K)/E(\mathbb Q)$ is finite. Choose an integer $m>0$ annihilating it,
and put $\Gamma=mE(K)\subset E(\mathbb Q)$. This group has positive rank.
Fix an integral Weierstrass equation and the parameter $\xi=-x/y$.
Ratios $\xi(U)/\xi(V)$ with $U,V\in\Gamma$ in the allowed affine chart
form a Diophantine subset $\mathscr A$ of $\mathbb Q^\times$.

For a nonzero rational target $a/b$, take integers $a,b$ with $b>0$.
Write the integral formal multiplication series as
$[n](z)=nz+z^2F_n(z)$. Whenever $v_p(z)>v_p(b)$, the exact identity

$$
\frac{\xi([a]Q)}{\xi([b]Q)}-\frac ab
 =
\frac{z\bigl(bF_a(z)-aF_b(z)\bigr)}
 {b\bigl(b+zF_b(z)\bigr)}
$$

gives

$$
v_p\!\left(\frac{\xi([a]Q)}{\xi([b]Q)}-\frac ab\right)
\ge v_p(z)-2v_p(b).
$$

Thus an arbitrarily deep common formal neighborhood supplies any finite
set of prescribed error bounds. The inverse denominator is legitimate:
$b+zF_b(z)$ has valuation exactly $v_p(b)$. Negative $a$ is permitted.
For target zero, first choose a nonzero rational number inside the desired
neighborhoods.

The intersection of these formal neighborhoods with $\Gamma$ has finite
index in $\Gamma$. Choose a nontorsion point $R$ in it. Compactness of
$E(\mathbb R)$ gives nonzero multiples $Q_j$ of $R$ tending to the
identity in the real topology. Every $Q_j$ remains in all the prescribed
nonarchimedean subgroups. The displayed ratio tends to $a/b$ at the real
place as well. Deep formal membership also ensures that the relevant
affine $x$ and $y$ coordinates are nonzero; nontorsion alone would not
imply $x\ne0$ on an arbitrary model.

Weak approximation first chooses a rational target in any prescribed
product of real and finite-prime neighborhoods, and the preceding
construction then approximates that target by $\mathscr A$. This proves
the required product density. Finite forbidden rational values are
avoided by choosing smaller neighborhoods disjoint from them. If the
original finite-prime list is empty, an auxiliary prime can be added.
No prescribed algebraic equality is inferred from this density.

## The prepared pole predicate

For $d\in K$, set

$$
P_d(v,s)=d(d+v^2)^2s^4-d(d-v^2)^2(1-d)^2.
$$

If a normalized nonarchimedean valuation has $n=v(d)<0$ odd, then
$r=v(d+v^2)=v(d-v^2)=\min(n,2v(v))$, with the evident interpretation
when the coordinate $v$ is zero. The two terms have valuations
$n+2r+4v(s)$ and $3n+2r$. They are both odd and cannot agree.
If $s=0$, the second term alone has odd valuation. Consequently
$Y^2=P_d(v,s)$ has no solution at such a valuation. This proof is valid
over every number field, permits zero coordinates, and does not assume
good reduction of an elliptic curve.

Let

$$
\mathcal M=\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm5,\pm6,\pm7,\pm10,\pm14\}.
$$

For $\mu\in\mathcal M$, define

$$
\Omega_\mu(b;v,s,Y)=
\mu^5Y^2-\mu^2b(b+\mu v^2)^2s^4
+b(b-\mu v^2)^2(\mu-b)^2.
$$

The exact identity
$\Omega_\mu=\mu^5(Y^2-P_{b/\mu}(v,s))$ proves its local implication
outside rational primes $\{2,3,5,7\}$. No nonzero guard on $b,v,s,Y$ is
needed, and $b=0$ is accepted by $Y=0$. Each fixed-$\mu$ polynomial has
degree nine, thirteen monomials, and integer content one.

For rational completeness it is enough to prepare a nonzero rational
$b$ with square reduced denominator and

$$
|b|<1,\qquad v_2(b)\ge5,\qquad
v_3(b),v_5(b),v_7(b)\ge1.
$$

There is a $\mu\in\mathcal M$ for which $\mu b>0$ and
$-\mu b\in\mathbb Q_2^{\times2}$. The eight positive absolute values of
$\mu$ represent all eight $2$-adic square classes, and the sign of $\mu$
is fixed by positivity. Then $d=b/\mu$ satisfies
$0<d<1$, $d\in7\mathbb Q_2^{\times2}$, and $v_2(d)\ge4$.
It still has square reduced denominator: at a prime dividing $\mu$ the
prepared valuations make $d$ integral, and elsewhere a negative
valuation remains even. These are precisely the prepared hypotheses
for the intrinsic-pole completeness theorem.

## Ordered completeness of the two tests

Put $g=f(t)$ for a rational input $t$ and

$$
f(T)=\frac{T^3-2}{T^3-3},\qquad
b_1=\frac{(\lambda-g)^2(1+g)^2}{g^3},\qquad
b_2=(\beta-t)^2
 \left(\frac{(1+\lambda)^2}{\lambda}\right)^{2k-1}.
$$

The chosen $f$ has no rational zero, pole, or value $-1$. First choose
$\lambda\in\mathscr A$ very close to $g$ at all numerator and
denominator primes of $g$, at $2,3,5,7$, and at the real place.
Avoid $\lambda=g$ and $\lambda=-1$. At the prime support of $g$, the
error can be made deep enough that $b_1$ is integral. Outside that
support its valuation is even whenever negative. The extra finite
conditions and real condition give all the displayed preparation
bounds for $b_1$.

Only after this choice is fixed, choose $\beta\in\mathscr A$ close to
$t$ at every numerator and denominator prime of $\lambda$, at the four
fixed primes, and really. Avoid $\beta=t$. If $e=v_p(\lambda)\ne0$,
then

$$
v_p\!\left(\frac{(1+\lambda)^2}{\lambda}\right)=-|e|.
$$

Arbitrarily deep approximation therefore makes $b_2$ integral at each
such prime and prepared at the four fixed primes. At all other primes
any negative valuation of $b_2$ is even. Real approximation gives
$0<|b_2|<1$. The intrinsic theorem supplies both pole triples.
The second pair of elliptic points is independent of the first; new
primes of the chosen $\lambda$ are not omitted.

## Local propagation and the global bound

Let the underlying rational prime be unramified in $K$, with every place
above it outside the pole predicate's fixed bad set. At one such place,
suppose $e=v(g)$ is nonzero and odd. With $h=v(\lambda-g)$ finite, the first test has valuation

$$
2h+2\min(0,e)-3e.
$$

It is odd, so the pole predicate forces it to be positive. For
$e=2j+1>0$, this gives $h\ge3j+2>e$. For
$e=-(2j+1)<0$, it gives $h\ge-j>e$. Hence $v(\lambda)=e$ in both
cases. If $\lambda=g$, that conclusion is immediate.

Since $\lambda$ is rational, the same odd value occurs at every place
of $K$ above the underlying unramified rational prime. The second test
then gives

$$
v(\beta-t)\ge(2k-1)j+k\ge k,\qquad |e|=2j+1.
$$

If $\beta=t$, the input is already rational. The value $\lambda=-1$
does not need an extra guard: the first test would have negative odd
valuation $4\min(0,e)-3e$ at every consumed prime, so no such prime
could occur.

For nonrational $t$, choose a conjugate $\sigma t\ne t$ in a fixed
Galois closure. The common rational $\beta$ and the preceding
all-places conclusion give valuation at least $k$ for
$t-\sigma t$ at every place above each consumed rational prime.
Exclude the finitely many ramified primes. After normalizing by field
degree, the sum of their logarithms is at most
$h(t-\sigma t)/k\le2h(t)/k+O(1)$. The odd divisor support in $K$ is
bounded by that same sum. The argument uses the counting conjecture
over $K$ only, not over its Galois closure.

For a reduced rational divisor $D$ of degree $r$ supported at odd-order
zeros or poles of $f$, outside fixed bad primes the parity of the
intersection multiplicity with $D$ equals the relevant valuation parity
of $f(t)$. The local inequality
$2\mathbf 1_{n>0}\le n+\mathbf 1_{n\text{ odd}}$ yields

$$
N_{D,\mathrm{odd}}(t)\ge
2N_D^{(1)}(t)-N_D(t)
\ge(2\theta-r)h(t)-O(1)
$$

under the stated lower bound $N_D^{(1)}\ge\theta h-O(1)$.
Thus $2\theta-r>2/k$ bounds the heights of nonrational solutions.
For the six-point divisor of the displayed cubic ratio, abc gives
$\theta=4-\epsilon$, and $k=2$ works for $\epsilon<1/2$.
For a degree-five polynomial divisor, $k=3$ works for
$\epsilon<1/6$. The checker verifies particular positive margins;
the all-parameter inequalities are mathematical proofs.

## Exceptional inputs, guards, and coordinate count

Northcott finiteness gives finitely many nonrational inputs in the
detector. The product $H\in\mathbb Q[T]$ of their minimal polynomials
has no rational roots. Adding $H(t)\ne0$ removes them while preserving
every rational input. This polynomial and the quantitative threshold
are ineffective; no computed exceptional set is claimed.

Four affine points on $E$ supply the two independent ratios. Standard
multiplication-map coordinate formulas have denominators supported on
$[m]^{-1}(O)$, so every preimage of an allowed image in $\Gamma^\circ$
lies in the permitted chart. All
multiplication-map, ratio, and contact-function denominators, the
required affine-chart factors, and $H(t)$ are combined in one inverse
equation. Each of the two intrinsic blocks uses precisely three
coordinates. Therefore the total is

$$
1+4\cdot2+2\cdot3+1=16.
$$

There are four curve equations, two pole-membership equations, and one
inverse equation. The sixteen choices of $\mu$ in a pole block can be
combined by a product after common denominator clearing; the two
products permit the choices independently. Alternatively there are
$16^2=256$ finite branches reusing exactly the same coordinates.
For an integer-coefficient single equation, choose a rational prime $p$
unramified in $K$. At each prime $\mathfrak p\mid p$ its normalized
valuation is one, so $Z^7-p$ is Eisenstein over $K_{\mathfrak p}$ and
irreducible over $K$. The power-basis norm polynomial for
$\mathbb Q(p^{1/7})$ has integer coefficients and remains anisotropic
over $K$. Substituting the seven integer residuals combines the system
without another coordinate. An ordinary sum of squares requires a real
embedding and is not valid over arbitrary $K$.

If $b_i=A_i/B_i$, multiplication of $\Omega_\mu(b_i)$ by $B_i^5$
gives the exact cleared residual

$$
\mu^5Y_i^2B_i^5-\mu^2A_i(A_i+\mu v_i^2B_i)^2s_i^4B_i^2
+A_i(A_i-\mu v_i^2B_i)^2(\mu B_i-A_i)^2.
$$

For $d=\max_i(\deg A_i,\deg B_i)$, its total degree is at most
$5d+8$. If the common guard has degree $g_0$, the final norm polynomial
has degree at most $7\max\{3,16(5d+8),g_0+1\}$. This bound depends on
the fixed multiplication map and on the ineffective exceptional
polynomial; it is not a universal numerical degree record.

The alternative power-shift correction is also valid. The earlier v69
avoidance lemma gives an integral $B$ such that the image of
$x\mapsto x^\ell+B$ avoids the finite irrational exceptional set.
For a prime $\ell$ with $\ell-1>[K:\mathbb Q]$, rationality of $x^\ell$
implies rationality of $x$. All rational inputs remain accepted, and
the same sixteen coordinates suffice. The degree increases by at most
the fixed factor $\ell$; the shift remains ineffective.

Composition with an $N$-coordinate definition of $\mathbb Z$ in
$\mathbb Q$ restricts all $N$ original coordinates, including the
distinguished input. It therefore uses $N+15N=16N$ coordinates over
$K$. An integral-basis expansion and elimination of its last coefficient
give $16N[K:\mathbb Q]$ for $\mathcal O_K$. The numbers $176$ and
$176[K:\mathbb Q]$ use the separate eleven-coordinate theorem and
retain all of that theorem's arithmetic premises. Restriction of abc
over $K$ to rational triples supplies ordinary rational abc, because
the degree-normalized radicals agree outside the finitely many ramified
primes. No other retained premise is thereby discharged.
