RIGOROUS RESTRICTED COST BOUND
Consider factors 1+sum q^k X^(2^e), where e is1 or2, with each of
the six arbitrary-valuation tests and ten even-valuation reciprocal tests
used once per detector. Require all term valuation classes to be disjoint
and avoid0, for every odd positive contact valuation nu. Let a arbitrary
tests and b even tests be fourth powers, and let m be the factor count.
Modulo8 the sixteen square terms initially occupy44 residue positions.
Taking fourth powers reduces that number by2a+b. A factor without an
even-test fourth power can occupy at most6 of the8 residues, because all
its classes have period at most4 and exclude0. A factor with such a term
can occupy at most7. Thus
 44-2a-b <=6m+min(b,m).
For m=5 this forces a+b>=7. For m=4 it forces a+b>=10. For m<=3 it is
impossible (a<=6,b<=10). Dividing a factor equation by a weight can remove
at most one fourth-power output: equal weight exponents give intersecting
valuation classes. Hence at least a+b-m shared field fourth-power outputs
remain. These output counts are for this literal power-sum model only;
they do not exclude norm expressions, new algebraic dependencies, different
local predicates, changes to the test list, or source-coordinate changes.

With d detector copies, factor and intermediate-product coordinates cost
d(4m-4), excluding final W coordinates present in either design. The shared
fourth-power outputs cost at least2 max(0,a+b-m). Ignoring the scalar-weight
cost and all order requirements, the minimum for d=2 is36, attained by the
current five-factor design; four factors also have relaxed lower bound36.
Thus merely reallocating squares/fourth powers in this restricted model
cannot lower the inherited228 count unless scalar-weight costs decrease or
some additional structure is used.
