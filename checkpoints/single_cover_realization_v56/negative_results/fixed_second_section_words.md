# Fixed words in the two-section trace family

Date: 17 November 2026. This concerns fixed rational section formulas,
not integer indices allowed to vary with the actual arithmetic tuple.

On `E_w: y²=x³−(2+24w)x²+(1+1176w)x`, take `w=r²−1` and
`P=(49,336)`, `Q=(25,120r)`. For the physical normalization
`w=4u/(1−u)²`, `r=(1+u)/(1−u)`, an odd physical contact has
`r≡1` or `−1` according as `v(u)>0` or `v(u)<0`.

At `w=0`, the nodal fiber is `y²=x(x−1)²`. On its smooth locus,
put `z=y/(x−1)` and use the multiplicative parameter

\[
                 h=(z+1)/(z-1),\qquad
                 x=((h+1)/(h-1))^2.
\]

At `r=1`, `P` has `h=4/3`, and `Q` has `h=3/2`. Thus the fixed
word `aP+bQ` specializes to

\[
                 h=2^{2a-b}3^{b-a}.
\]

At `r=−1`, replace `b` by `−b`. The actual trace is
`V=(x−1)/24`; residues `V=1` and `V=2` correspond precisely to

\[
                 h\in\{3/2,2/3,4/3,3/4\}.
\]

Multiplicative independence of 2 and 3, and the determinant-one
exponent matrix `[[2,−1],[−1,1]]`, show that the specialized trace
is identically one or two only for the four words `±P`, `±Q`.
For any other fixed nonzero word, its specialized trace is a fixed
rational number different from one and two. It can reduce to one
or two only at finitely many primes, namely primes dividing the
two fixed difference numerators (with the usual finite denominator
and bad-reduction exclusions). The zero word specializes to infinity
and supplies no finite trace.

Consequently a fixed finite menu of other words cannot match actual
reciprocal detector traces at arbitrarily large selected contacts.
For example `P+Q` has trace `1/3` at `r=1` and trace `12` at
`r=−1`; `P−Q` interchanges these values. Neither is congruent to
one or two at any prime greater than 11.

This argument leaves open unbounded, tuple-dependent integer words.
It also does not identify every section of the elliptic surface with
an integral word in `P,Q`; no assertion about the full generic
Mordell–Weil group is used.
