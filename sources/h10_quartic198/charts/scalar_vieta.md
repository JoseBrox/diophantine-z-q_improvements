# Scalar chart proposal: exact local result, global compiler pending

The scalar block in the 204-coordinate source has seven field coordinates and the additional two rational components of the root ordinate carrier j_S. The following chart replaces all eight field coordinates by six field coordinates and one rational nonzero guard. If the remaining scalar and comparison circuits can be homogenized compatibly, the net saving is three rational coordinates.

Let K=Q(sqrt(2)), D=11-6sqrt(2). Use u,v,w,m,k,p in K and impose

p=uv,
k^2=pw,
u+v+w+2m+Dk^2=0,
p+w(u+v)=m^2.

Write k=k_0+k_1 sqrt(2) and impose k_0*j=1 with j rational. Thus k is nonzero. These are eight rational quadratic equations and one additional rational quadratic equation. Every solution yields

U=(1/u,(m+u)/(ku)),
V=(1/v,-(m+v)/(kv)),
Q=U-V=(1/w,-(m+w)/(kw)).

The proof is the Vieta cubic identity

a(m+a)^2+Dk^2a^2=k^2 (a=u,v,w),

and the line k*y=m*x+1 through U,-V,-Q. The nonzero product uvw=k^2 excludes all zero abscissas. Repeated roots are tangencies and need no division by their differences.

Given nonzero reference S=y(H)/x(H) and T=y(H-P)/x(H-P), enforce the scaled comparison labels a=2*nu(Q) via

L*(m+u)=2*k*S,
LP*(m+v)=-2*k*T.

The scalar quotient Z=2*z(Q)/z(H) is enforced by

Z*(m+w)=-2*k*S.

All three equations are field quadrics. Their nonzero right sides automatically exclude zero ordinates of U,V,Q; Q is therefore not torsion (all K-rational torsion is two-torsion under the retained group premise). Its label a=2*nu(Q) is a nonzero integer.

The scalar's odd-prime valuations are unaffected by replacing its old label a and quotient z(Q)/z(H) by the new integer label 2*nu(Q) and quotient 2*z(Q)/z(H). Exact numerical constants and the 2-adic preparation must nevertheless be checked; this is not yet a theorem that the stored scalar circuit has the required projection.

The ratio equations also expose the k=0 obstruction. Without the nonzero guard, u=v, w=0, m=-u, p=u^2, k=0 gives a collapsed stratum where the point pair no longer represents finite Q. Merely declaring the displayed rational formulas does not remove it.

## Height aggregate

One can avoid an extra reciprocal of w by using the native quadratic function w^2=1/x(Q)^2 in the trace aggregate, for example

T=5+(1/4)Tr[x(Q1)+x(Q3)+x(Q4)+1/x(Q2)^2].

At either embedding x(Qi)>=-d for finite Qi, so T>=5-18/4>0. The second term is nonnegative and is defined since Q2 has nonzero abscissa. It is a quadratic expression in all stored coordinates: each recovered xi for i=1,3,4 is s_i^2+D(f_i+g_i), and 1/x(Q2)^2=w^2.

The existing weighted-trace height proof extends to this mixed function. In the fixed E0 model, x has double poles at O, and 1/x^2 has fourth-order poles at the rational two-torsion point (0,0). After pullback along a nonconstant elliptic direction, a shared highest-order pole among fourth-order summands has a nonzero coefficient which is a common nonzero complex scalar times a sum of positive rational fourth powers of ratios of the nonzero indices, multiplied by fixed positive real factors 1/d^2 or 1/d'^2 within the two conjugate blocks. Double poles cannot cancel a fourth-order principal part. If there are no fourth-order poles in the chosen direction, the original positive-double-pole argument applies. Independence of the two conjugate generators prevents a pole shared across the two blocks. Freeze index-zero torsion coordinates as in Brox; in the scalar coordinate those cases have already been excluded. The same family-height theorem then gives max |nu(Qi)|^2 <= A*h(T)+J, with a fixed ineffective J.

The chart's component restriction k0!=0 is a genuine extra open-domain condition, stronger than k!=0. It is compatible with completeness by finite avoidance. Fix a nonzero scalar point Q=[a]R0 with x(Q)!=0 and parametrize U=[a]W, V=U-Q, where W=[h]R0. The reciprocal secant intercept k is a nonconstant rational function of U: as U approaches O, the secant intercept has principal term x(Q)/z(U), so k approaches zero, while k is generically finite and nonzero. Its pullback under the nonconstant isogeny [a] is also nonconstant. On E times its conjugate, the rational component is (k(first)+sigma(k)(second))/2, a nonzero rational function because each summand is nonconstant. The cyclic group generated by (R0,sigma(R0)) is Zariski dense by the conjugate-generator independence proved in Brox. By Mordell--Lang a proper subvariety meets this cyclic group in finitely many points: otherwise it contains a translate of a nonzero subgroup of the cyclic group, which is finite index and again Zariski dense. Thus only finitely many reference integers violate k0!=0. The source's CRT and compact-group recurrence supply infinitely many suitable reference integers, so these finitely many are avoided. This uses the already established independence and the classical Mordell--Lang theorem, rather than a new arithmetic conjecture.

An independent audit in audit/check_scalar_vieta.py verifies the algebraic identities. Audit206 independently accepted the mixed-pole height argument and this finite-avoidance proof. The exact scalar circuit rescaling is still pending: in the 212-source indices, L2 is stored as R times the true comparison ratio, while Z in slots 107,108 is stored as the true scalar ratio divided by R. The unscaled Vieta equations above therefore do not yet compile without either changing these weights globally or adding an intermediate field product. This can reduce the proposed saving from three to one rational coordinate. No final variable count is asserted here.
