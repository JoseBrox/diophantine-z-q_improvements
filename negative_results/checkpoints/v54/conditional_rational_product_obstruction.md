# Rational detector products in the conditional source

Date: 16 November 2026. Private checkpoint created while the workspace executor was unavailable.

## Scope

This result concerns the preferred conditional source with
\[
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),\qquad
F(t)=\frac{t(t+1)(2t+1)(3t+1)(4t+1)(5t+2)}{120},
\quad
w(t)=\frac{F(t)}{4(t-1)^8},
\]
\[
W(t)=\prod_{i=1}^6(1+C_i^2/F(t)),\qquad
b=4w/\operatorname{Tr}(W^{-1})^2.
\]
The mandatory tests include
\[
C_1=f(H),\qquad C_3=\overline{f(H-P)},\qquad f=1/x,
\]
on the source curve \(y^2=x^3-(3-\sqrt2)^2x\), with
\[
P=\left((57-28\sqrt2)/8,\;(-660+417\sqrt2)/32\right).
\]
The other tests and every point/difference guard are retained. The exceptional set S is fixed and contains all primes at most 76176.

The statement applies to an actual rational source tuple for which the chosen finite square criterion has already supplied the soundness property that b has no negative odd valuation outside S. It applies to the published R_a family and to the privately proved moving-positive-prefactor family. It is not an obstruction to arbitrary rational witnesses with nonrational W.

## Proposition: a rational W excludes every nonexceptional odd contact

Suppose W belongs to Q at such a tuple. Then F(t) has even valuation at every prime outside S.

Proof. If v_p(F(t)) is odd outside S, then t is p-integral and t-1 is a unit. Indeed a pole of t gives the even valuation 6v_p(t), and t congruent to1 gives F(t) congruent to7, a unit. Thus
\[
\nu=v_p(F(t))=v_p(w)>0
\]
is odd.

Since W is rational, the reciprocal trace is \(2/W\) and
\[
b=wW^2.
\]
The assumed exclusion of negative odd b gives
\[
\nu+2v_p(W)\ge1,\qquad v_p(W)>-\nu/2.
\]

At either place above p, every detector factor has valuation
\[
\min\{0,\,2v(C_i)-\nu\}\le0,
\]
because the second order is odd and cannot tie the constant order. Since W is rational, the sum of these factor valuations is the same \(v_p(W)\) at both places. Each individual factor valuation is at least that sum. Consequently
\[
v(C_i)>\nu/4>0
\]
at both places, for every i.

In particular, at one fixed place above p, f(H) has positive valuation, and the conjugate-slot assertion for \(C_3\) gives positive valuation for f(H-P) at that same place. Both H and H-P therefore belong to the formal group. Their difference P also belongs to it.

This is impossible: outside S the displayed curve has good reduction and P is an affine integral point, since its only coordinate denominators are powers of2. Its reduction is not the identity. The contradiction excludes the odd contact. The same argument works at an inert prime, where the conjugate valuation is the same valuation. QED.

No assumption that H is fixed, or that it equals P/2, was used.

## Corollary: both blocks cannot give uniform completeness by making W rational

The possible nonzero rational arguments t with F(t) of even valuation outside S lie in a finite set.

Indeed their square classes belong to the finite group generated by -1 and the primes of S. For each representative d, the equation
\[
F(t)=d y^2
\]
defines a fixed smooth genus-two curve, since F has six distinct roots. Faltings's theorem gives finitely many rational points on each of these finitely many twists. Let T be the resulting finite set of allowed rational arguments, excluding the guarded zero and contact arguments.

If both actual blocks have rational W and satisfy the finite square criterion, then
\[
t_0=\lambda\in T,\qquad t_1=a_1\lambda\in T.
\]
Hence
\[
a_1\in\{t'/t:t,t'\in T,\ t\ne0\},
\]
a finite set.

In the conditional source
\[
a_1=(X^2+B)^d+\operatorname{Tr}(x(A))^d,\qquad d\in\{4,6\},
\]
with d even and fixed B. Therefore \(a_1\ge(X^2+B)^d\) is unbounded on integer inputs. Only finitely many integer inputs can have successful tuples with rational W in both blocks. This strategy cannot prove completeness for all integers or the desired nine-variable bound.

The result uses Faltings only for this finite-argument consequence. The local impossibility at an odd contact is elementary.

## Limits of the conclusion

* Rational W in just one block forces that block's argument into the same finite set; it does not by itself rule out a construction using a nonrational product in the other block.
* The argument is stated for the conditional source. In the general source the extra delta denominator can suppress the mandatory field tests at scalar-support primes; the conditional proof cannot be transplanted there without a separate audit.
* This does not exclude rational points on proper ramified incidence covers with \(W\notin\mathbb Q\), and does not exclude 21/9 by another construction.
* No new variable bound or actual global rational realization has been proved.
