# Rational quadratic twists of the original carrier: an obstruction to rank stability

Let F=Q(sqrt(2)), d=3-sqrt(2), and E_n:y^2=x^3-n^2x. The original project carrier is E_d. For every a in Q^* the curve E_{ad} is its quadratic twist by a.

**Theorem.** The 2-infinity Selmer corank of E_{ad}/F is odd for every rational a!=0. In particular, if the 2-primary Tate--Shafarevich group of E_{ad}/F is finite, then rank E_{ad}(F) is odd and positive. Therefore under that finiteness hypothesis no quadratic K/Q disjoint from F has rank E_d(KF)=rank E_d(F). This is a genuine obstruction to the previously proposed carrier-specific rank-stability route, not a proof of the Sha-finiteness premise.

Independently of Sha, the exact rank of E_{ad}(F) is one for a=1,3,5,7,15,17,21,35. It follows that rank E_d(KF)=2 for K=Q(sqrt(a)) with a equal to any of the seven nontrivial listed positive integers or their negatives or their doubles or negative doubles. Also rank E_d(F(i))=2. The fixed curve thus fails rank stability unconditionally in these examples. No rank claim for other twists is inferred from an upper bound or root number.

## 1. Descent notation

For the isogeny phi:E_n -> E'_n:y^2=x^3+4n^2x with kernel {O,(0,0)}, let I,I' be the local or global x-coordinate Kummer images on E_n,E'_n. At O the class is 1 and at (0,0) it is the constant linear coefficient -n^2 or 4n^2. The covering for a squareclass b is

    N^2=b U^4+(B/b)V^4,    B=-n^2 or B=4n^2,

including U=0 and V=0. This follows by x=b(U/V)^2 and y=b U N/V^3. The local ratio |coker(phi)|/|ker(phi)| is |I'|/2. The composition of the two dual isogenies is [2] up to a fixed isomorphism; since E_n has all its two-torsion rational, the standard global two-isogeny index formula is

    2^(rank+2)=|I_global| |I'_global|.

For example, derive it by factoring E_n(F)/2E_n(F) through the image of the dual isogeny. The nonidentity dual-kernel point (0,0) on E'_n is already the image of (n,0) on E_n, so there is no missing factor of two. The identical argument over a finite completion gives |I| |I'|=|E_n(F_v)/2E_n(F_v)|. The latter equals 4 |2|_v^(-1) by the local elliptic group and the index of [2] on a sufficiently deep formal subgroup. In particular the product is 4 at odd residue characteristic and 16 at the dyadic completion F_v=Q_2(sqrt(2)).

## 2. Local images and the global ratio

At an odd finite place with even v(n), rescale by a square to make n a unit. Good reduction gives I and I' the order-two unramified squareclass line: the images are unramified and the residual isogeny cokernels have size two, by equality of point counts. At a place with odd v(n), scale to v(n)=1. If -1 is nonsquare, I contains the independent classes -1 and n from the two-torsion; hence I is the full four-element squareclass group and I' is trivial by the product identity. If -1 is square, fix i in the completion. The identity (1+i)^4=-4 gives an isomorphism E_n -> E'_n scaling abscissas by the square (1+i)^2. Hence the images coincide and have size two by the product identity; both are the line generated by n. This proves all local cases needed here.

At either real place, E'_n has nonnegative abscissas and the exceptional class 4n^2 is positive. Thus I' is trivial there.

At the dyadic completion, rational a modulo squares is represented by 1, -1, 5 or -5, since 2 is already a square. The sign of n has no effect on either curve. Thus it suffices to test n=d and n=5d. The following exact finite computation is supplied in verify.py. Write pi=sqrt(2) and O_v=Z_2[pi]. A unit is A+B pi with A odd. Hensel's lemma (v_pi(2)=2) implies every 1+pi^5 O_v element is a square. Therefore the unit squareclasses can be read from the ring O_v/(8), whose square subgroup among its 32 units consists of the four pairs (1,0),(1,4),(3,2),(3,6). The eight representatives are

    (1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(3,1),(3,3),(5,0),(5,2).

Adjoin the odd valuation representatives by multiplying by pi. For E_n, n is a dyadic unit so a nonzero abscissa has even valuation (if negative the cube term dominates, if positive the linear term dominates); only unit representatives are needed. For E'_n test both valuation parities. For each b, B/b is integral locally, and a local quartic solution may be normalized with one of U,V a unit equal to 1 and the other integral; then N is integral. Reduction modulo 32 is a necessary test. Enumerate all fourth powers and all squares in (Z/32Z)[pi]/(pi^2-2). In each of the two normalized charts test whether b times the fourth power plus B/b (or vice versa) belongs to the square set. This is a complete finite exclusion test, not a sampling of local points. For both n=d and 5d the upper bounds are 8 for I and 2 for I', with I' contained in the classes 1,5. Their product equals the proven local index 16, hence the bounds are exact. In particular the dyadic ratio |I'|/2 equals 1 for every rational twist.

Now scale a by a rational square and change its sign if necessary. Removing its factor 2 is legitimate over F since 2 is a square. We can assume a is a positive odd squarefree integer. At the two primes above 7 (d and its conjugate, of norm 7), exactly one has v(ad) odd: for 7 not dividing a these valuations are 1,0; for 7 dividing a they are 2,1. Since -1 is nonsquare in F_7, the product of the two local ratios is 1/2. Every other prime dividing a is either inert, in which case the residue size is p^2 and -1 is square, or split. For split primes p=1 mod 8, -1 is square; for split primes p=7 mod 8 it is nonsquare at both places, and their product is 1/4. All other finite odd ratios are 1. The two real places contribute (1/2)^2. Thus if s is the number of rational primes p!=7, p=7 mod 8 dividing a, then

    product_v |coker(phi_v)|/|ker(phi_v)| = 2^(-3-2s).

The valuation is odd. Apply the Cassels--Fisher isogeny parity formula for the 2-infinity Selmer corank (e.g. T. and V. Dokchitser, *Parity of ranks for elliptic curves with a cyclic isogeny*, J. Number Theory 128 (2008), pp. 662--679, Section 1, local sigma product; this formula precedes and does not require their later local root-number theorem). It gives odd corank. Finiteness of the 2-primary Sha group identifies the corank with the Mordell--Weil rank. Equivalently under finiteness one can use the isogeny Selmer-size formula and the alternating Cassels--Tate pairing: the dual-isogeny Sha-kernel ratio is a square, so the parity of the product of the two global image sizes equals the parity of the local ratio. We do not equate 2-Selmer rank with Mordell--Weil rank without such justification.

For K=Q(sqrt(a)) disjoint from F, decompose E_d(FK) tensor Q into the plus and minus eigenspaces for Gal(FK/F). The plus space comes from E_d(F), the minus space from E_{ad}(F) by twisting. This proves the rank sum, and the stated conditional obstruction. The twist by -1 is isomorphic to E_d over F (the coefficients depend on n^2); the twist by 2 is also isomorphic over F. Twisting by 7 gives the conjugate curve up to a square, since d*(3+sqrt(2))=7. These give useful checks on the obstruction.

## 3. Exact unconditional ranks for eight twists

The ring of integers of F is Z[sqrt(2)], of class number one by the Minkowski bound sqrt(8)/2<2. Its units are generated by -1 and eps=1+sqrt(2): there is no unit strictly between 1 and eps in the first real embedding since its conjugate is plus or minus its inverse, so the integral coefficient of sqrt(2) would have absolute value strictly between zero and one. Thus global Kummer squareclasses are represented by -1, eps and squarefree products of prime generators dividing B. To see the support restriction, at a finite prime not dividing B a positive valuation of x makes the linear term dominate and a negative valuation makes the cube dominate; either forces even valuation. The two exceptional Kummer classes also have the claimed support.

For the eight twists tested, all odd prime generators are d,3+sqrt(2),3,5,5+2sqrt(2),5-2sqrt(2) as appropriate. The rational primes 3,5 are inert, and the latter two pairs split 7 and 17 by the norm identities. The dual support also includes sqrt(2). Enumerate the squarefree products and reject the dual classes not positive at both real places, all classes excluded by the odd local table of Section 2, and the remaining classes excluded by the dyadic quartic residue test. It leaves eight classes for I and only 1 for I' for each listed a. Thus rank <=1 without a Sha hypothesis.

The verifier substitutes every point exactly. Each has nonzero ordinate and is non-torsion: for a!=17 the two good primes over 17 give point counts 16 and 20 in some order, so prime-to-17 torsion has order dividing four; the good prime above 11 eliminates 17-primary torsion (its order is prime to 17). For a=17 the good primes above 3 and 5 have orders 16 and 20, eliminating all odd torsion and bounding the 2-primary part by four. Since the full two-torsion already has order four, these bounds identify the entire torsion group. This proves exact rank one in every row.

Reproduce using python3 verify.py. The check uses only Python integer arithmetic, enumerates every relevant residue and squareclass, and verifies the points and torsion bounds. It does not use floating point, analytic rank, PARI, or a Sha-finiteness assumption for the eight rank computations. The conditional uniform parity statement invokes the stated published isogeny formula. This proves the base carrier rank independently, but not the exact saturation identity 2E_d(F)=Z P for the project's particular P. The latter remains an inherited input where needed. The earlier carrier-specific rank-stability theorem is a valid conditional implication, but its rank hypothesis is now known to be incompatible with the expected Sha finiteness for every disjoint quadratic field.
