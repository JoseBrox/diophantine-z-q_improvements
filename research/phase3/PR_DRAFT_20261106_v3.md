# Draft for review: direct Lucas tests and exact polynomial specifications

The existing shared-point construction retains rational proxies solely
to transfer an integer Lucas relation through prime-power comparisons.
Testing the same polynomial directly on the logarithmic ratios removes
those proxies and two comparison tests while preserving the conditional
arithmetic argument.

This proposed research addition supplies:

- A 41-variable indexed polynomial and a 43-variable semilocal alternative,
  with proved degree and coefficient bounds and independent specifications.
- Complete quartic tables with 1315 and 1335 variables; the latter has
  ordinary integer coefficient height below `2^1020`.
- A rational affine change reducing the 1315-variable quartic's exact
  primitive coefficient height to `64*3^(2^28)`, with unchanged degree.
- Separately checked input-shift-2402 versions of the indexed formulas.
- Unconditional lower bounds for finite systems, precise obstructions to
  proposed further compression methods, and a literature review.
- Exact counterexamples to unchanged number-field base extension, plus
  degree-72 division-norm encodings of the quadratic systems.
- A fresh standalone TeX paper and reproducible exact verification scripts.

The 2–10-variable objective remains unresolved. The interpretations as
definitions of `Z` over `Q` remain conditional on the explicitly named
source arithmetic inputs and Sun's simultaneous prime-pattern theorem.
No new theorem defining the ring of integers in every number field is
claimed. Algebraic certificate checks are separated from these arithmetic
dependencies throughout the report.

The released polynomial, manuscript, preceding source packages, and
generic checkers are unchanged. All new work is under `research/phase3/`.
The working tree also contains the earlier review work under `research/`.

Validation: four indexed specifications, five complete sparse quartics,
seven cyclic-norm specifications, explicit contact/cancellation/anchor
counterexamples, independent fixed-point calculations, and static TeX
consistency checks. Large indexed determinants are not expanded; no PDF
rendering or completed formal proof is claimed.
