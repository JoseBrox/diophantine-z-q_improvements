# Sources for the second-stage lower bounds

All mathematical assertions in `results.tex` are independent of the proposed
arithmetic definition in the repository. The geometric proofs are written
out, including the degeneracies in intersections of two quadrics. References
are used only for the specific inputs identified below.

## Essential inputs inherited from the first report

- János Kollár, *Unirationality of cubic hypersurfaces*, J. Inst. Math.
  Jussieu **1** (2002), 467–476, Theorem 2.
  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0005146>;
  DOI <https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748002000117>.
  The theorem is used only over a perfect field, in dimension at least two,
  for a geometrically integral cubic that is not a cone and has a rational
  point. Cubic cones are handled separately by a birational product with a
  curve; geometrically reducible components are never passed to this theorem.
- Serge Lang, *Fundamentals of Diophantine Geometry* (Springer, 1983),
  Chapter 8, pp. 188–224.
  <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-1810-2>.
  Siegel's theorem is applied to a nonconstant rational function on a
  positive-genus projective curve. Removing its poles gives an affine curve
  finite over the affine line. After enlarging a fixed finite set of primes,
  its coordinates are integral over the ring generated by that function;
  hence integral values imply points integral outside that fixed set.
- The original degree-at-most-three obstruction and one-witness Hilbert
  irreducibility argument are included in the standalone wrapper from
  `../../optimization/paper_parts/lower_bounds.tex`. Its bibliography is
  included without modification.

## Additional verified primary sources

- D. Eisenbud, M. Green, K. Hulek and S. Popescu, *Small schemes and
  varieties of minimal degree*, American J. Math. **128** (2006), no. 6,
  1363–1389, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1353/ajm.2006.0043>.
  Author's full text:
  <https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/2006-002.pdf>.
  The first page explicitly recalls
  `degree(X) >= 1 + codimension(X, span(X))` for an integral projective
  variety. This is the only result imported from the paper; the classification
  of varieties of minimal degree is not needed. Bibliographic data were also
  checked against the coauthor's institutional publication record:
  <https://www.iag.uni-hannover.de/en/forschung/publikationen/publikation-detail/publications/small-schemes-and-varieties-of-minimal-degree>.
- J. Harris and Y. Tschinkel, *Rational points on quartics*, Duke Math. J.
  **104** (2000), no. 3, 477–500.
  Author's full text:
  <https://cims.nyu.edu/~tschinke/papers/yuri/00quart/quart.pdf>;
  original preprint <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9809015>.
  The author's publication list confirms the journal data:
  <https://cims.nyu.edu/~tschinke/publications.html>.
  Conjecture 1.1 states weak Lang. Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 distinguish potential
  density from density over the given field. They are cited as context for
  the remaining quartic-surface obstruction, not as a theorem that all K3
  surfaces have dense rational points or real approximation.
- J. Berg, C. Pagano, B. Poonen, M. Stoll, N. Triantafillou, B. Viray
  and I. Vogt, *Brauer–Manin obstructions requiring arbitrarily many Brauer
  classes*, Bull. London Math. Soc. **56** (2024), no. 5, 1587–1604,
  DOI <https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12999>.
  Author's full text:
  <https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/many_brauer.pdf>.
  Theorem 5.1 gives the Faddeev residue sequence and its
  sum-of-corestrictions relation. In characteristic zero Tsen's theorem
  makes its relative Brauer group the entire Brauer group of K(T).
  A class with zero finite residues therefore has zero residue at infinity
  and is constant. Section 6.1 records the correspondence between conics
  and quaternion classes used here. The published journal page confirms
  the metadata:
  <https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/blms.12999>.
- J.-P. Serre, *Quelques applications du théorème de densité de Chebotarev*,
  Publ. Math. IHÉS **54** (1981), 123–201,
  DOI <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02698692>.
  Full text:
  <https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1981__54__123_0.pdf>.
  Section 2.1, Theorem 1 gives the positive density of every conjugacy
  class in a finite Galois extension of number fields. The conic argument
  selects an element fixing a residue field L but negating a nonsquare
  square root. Its Frobenius primes give a degree-one prime of L and a
  nonsquare residue. The construction, including its exclusion of finitely
  many bad primes, is written out in `conic_obstruction.tex`.
- J. Voight, *Identifying the matrix ring: algorithms for quaternion
  algebras and quadratic forms*, author's revised text of 21 May 2019;
  original preprint arXiv:1004.0994.
  Full text: <https://jvoight.github.io/articles/quatalgs-051919.pdf>;
  preprint record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0994>.
  Proposition 5.5 verifies the odd-residue-characteristic local Hilbert
  symbol criterion. Together with bilinearity this gives exactly the tame
  square-class formula used for specializations of quaternion classes.
  The 2019 date describes the inspected author's revision, not a claimed
  journal publication date.

## The conic specialization result

The complete argument is in `conic_obstruction.tex`, and the main agent
has independently reviewed its delicate steps. It proves a simultaneous
dichotomy for a finite collection of affine conics over K(T), including
degenerate conics. If one has a generic affine point, its projection is
cofinite in K. Otherwise every integral arithmetic progression contains
infinitely many specializations at which all members lack affine points.
The smooth cases use the three primary sources above; rank-two nonsplit
cases use the integral form of Hilbert irreducibility already cited in
the first lower-bound report. No historical-priority claim is made for
this consequence of classical tools.

## What has not been claimed

The strengthened five-total-variable bound applies to systems of quadratics
and their anisotropic quadratic combinations, including positive sums of
squares. The degree-eight bound in three total variables assumes global
nonnegativity. Neither proves that an arbitrary sign-changing quartic needs
more than two existential witnesses. The weak Bombieri–Lang application is
explicitly conditional. The conic result excludes joint witness degree at
most two for two-witness definitions at every input degree, but does not
exclude generic fibers of positive genus or general quartic K3 surfaces.
