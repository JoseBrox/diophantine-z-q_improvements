## Linear valuation extraction

For Phi_rho(C)=rho*C/(1+rho*C), positive valuation implies v(rho*C)>0 and then v(Phi)=v(rho)+v(C). This remains valid when C=0 by assigning valuation infinity.

At a comparison-contact prime let mu=v(rho) be even, nu=v(R)>0 odd, and alpha=mu+nu.     v(C)>(5mu+9nu)/4.

If mu>=0 this is>9/4, hence v(C)>=3. If mu<0 is even, alpha>0 implies nu>=3, and the lower bound equals nu+5alpha/4>nu; it is again at least3. No negative-even valuation stratum is discarded.

If mu is odd, the disjoint contact sets and the scale (1+rho T^2)^2 give one R unit as before. The unchanged scalar normalization with K=A=5 gives v(S)>17mu/8, hence v(c)>=2mu+1>=3. No comparison-factor integrality is assumed in this soundness step.

Inverse ordinates are sufficient formal tests. At a good prime, v(1/y(Q))>0 implies v(y)<0 and the curve equation forces v(x)<0, with v(x)=−2r and v(y)=−3r for r>=1. ## Nonvanishing of the linear denominators

Fix the integer labels before prime selection, and on E write q_n(H)=z([n]H)/z(H). This is nonconstant for |n|>1: otherwise z composed with [n] would have the same degree as z, contradicting deg[n]=n^2. For n=1 or−1 the function is respectively1 or−1.

Over an algebraic closure, Res_{K/Q}E is E times its conjugate, with independent factors. Thus each paired comparison

    q_n(H_1)−q_n^sigma(H_2−P^sigma)

is nonconstant for |n|>1, and is zero for n=1 or−1. Its denominator1+rho*C cannot vanish identically. The inverse-ordinate comparisons tend to zero near the relevant identity or translated identity, so their linear denominators are nonzero rational functions. The target comparison tends to n−beta=0 at H=O in completeness. The Lucas comparison tends to F(a,t,b)=0 there. Their denominators are also not identically zero. This argument is on the abelian surface; it does not assert a false one-variable identity between the conjugate factors.

## Avoidance inside prepared real and finite neighborhoods

Let Gamma be the cyclic subgroup of Res E generated by P. Its geometric Zariski closure is the full abelian surface: any proper subgroup would give, after identifying the two elliptic factors with E_0, a nonzero endomorphism relation between the conjugate points modulo torsion, contrary to the proved independence lemma.

Every finite-index subgroup q*Gamma, and every coset, is likewise Zariski dense. Let C be its compact real closure. This closed subgroup has finitely many connected components. The Zariski closure of its identity component is the whole surface: otherwise the finite union of its translates could not be dense in an irreducible surface. Each component is therefore Zariski dense.

Every nonempty relatively open subset U of a component is Zariski dense as well. If an algebraic closed subset contained U, its local real-analytic equations would vanish on an open set and then on the connected component by analytic continuation. The component is Zariski dense, so the subset would be the whole surface. Since the cyclic coset is dense in its real closure, the cyclic points in U are also Zariski dense.

Apply this to the real neighborhood already preserving the scalar inequalities, and to the fixed index congruence that preserves all finite-place scalar bounds. Choose a cyclic point in that slice avoiding every linear denominator and every existing rational-chart denominator. These are finitely many proper algebraic zero sets. After that choice, shrink real and finite-place neighborhoods about the chosen point and refine the index congruence so all comparison normalizations are bounded. Only then select lambda and the contact primes. This is the necessary order: one does not demand that a preselected point avoid the new denominators automatically.
